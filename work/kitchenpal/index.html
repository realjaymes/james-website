The Finding

A reviewer wrote that the app "absolutely is not free if you actually want it to be useful" and called the free-app advertising false advertising. Your team replied the next day: "we're sorry that it wasn't clear that we have a paywall scan feature that is limited to only 100 scans per user." Read the full plan →

The business logic in that reply is completely reasonable. The first half of it is also an admission that the limit was not communicated before she hit it. She did not find a generous free tier with a fair cap. She found out mid-task that a feature she expected to work needed payment. That is a sequencing problem, not a pricing or product problem, and it is fixable without touching a single feature.

The Fix Uses Copy You Already Wrote

Your Play Store description already lists the premium features explicitly, which most freemium apps skip. The trouble is that almost nobody reads an app description end to end before installing. They install, hit the wall mid-task, and are not primed to read fine print. The information exists. It is not surfaced where someone needs it. See the three moves and the measurement plan →

Three moves: state the free limit before anyone reaches it, replace the hard block with a paywall that remembers what the person was doing, and give the recipe-search wall its own notice, since search is a core expected feature in a way a scan quota is not. Then a usage-triggered email lifecycle, because a permanent free tier has no deadline to build a sequence around.
