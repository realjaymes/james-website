A demo, a deck and a plan

Putting a Price on What AI Can't Read

James Praise · Marketing and GTM Operator

This is a working demo, a customer deck and a go-to-market plan. The demo runs on an illustrative dataset for a fictional JavaScript-heavy store called Meridian Outfitters. A real customer's numbers would come from their own Google Analytics and Prerender's render logs.

The idea

AI recommends what it can read

Buyers now ask ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity what to buy, and the crawlers behind those tools fetch pages without running JavaScript. Vercel and MERJ's crawler study found that none of the major AI crawlers render it, across hundreds of millions of fetches. A modern store can rank well on Google and still serve AI a blank page.

Prerender already fixes this for search engines, and its AI Insights work shows the same render layer reaching AI crawlers. A dashboard can turn that position into something a buyer can see and fund. It tells one story at three depths. A technical SEO reads which crawlers fail on which pages. A marketing lead reads the mentions slipping to a competitor. A CMO reads a revenue number.

Monitoring tools can show a brand going invisible. Prerender serves the page, so it can show the gap and close it.

What I built

The live demo

I built a clickable demo of the dashboard in plain HTML with no engineering help. It has four tabs: Overview, Discoverability, AI Citations and a Board Digest.

The revenue math is simple enough to say out loud. Sessions on pages AI can't read, multiplied by revenue per visit from the customer's Google Analytics, multiplied by 12, gives revenue at risk. Recoverable is the share of those pages Prerender can render.

The customer deck

The four-slide customer deck carries one insight from the first meeting to the CMO business case. Each slide answers the question the last one raises.

Slide 4 sets out the three real options: do nothing, monitor only, or render the pages. Doing nothing and monitoring both leave the pages unreadable.

The go-to-market plan

Sales gets three assets, built in the order a deal needs them.

1. The narrative deckUsed from the first meeting to the CMO business case. It sets the message the other two build on.
2. Battlecard and qualification guideNeeded the moment real calls start, with comparisons to Botify, Peec AI, Otterly.AI, Semrush and Ahrefs, and answers to the objections each one raises.
3. ROI calculatorNeeded last, at the budget stage. Buyers type in their own conversion rate, traffic and order value, so it needs no integration to run.

Before the deck is built, I'd test its core message in 5 to 8 short buyer interviews across technical SEO, marketing and CMO roles.
