A brief for WP Engine

Category-Positioning Brief: The Agent-Ready Web

James Praise · Product Marketing Operator

Built entirely from WP Engine's own public surfaces: wpengine.com, developers.wpengine.com, the DECODE 2026 keynote microsite, and press coverage, plus Automattic's and Hostinger's public competing claims.

The brief

The stakes, reframed

WP Engine's DECODE 2026 keynote already contains a real category argument, a Four-Problem Framework: creation paralysis (siloed AI tools lacking brand context), a discovery black hole (content invisible to LLMs), agentic incompatibility (sites invisible to AI agents making purchasing decisions), and an AI experience gap (users expecting conversational, personalized experiences). The keynote's own line states it plainly: "Your site is no longer static. You now have a new audience, agents whose patterns are not predictable."

4real problems already framed in WP Engine's own DECODE keynote
1place that framework currently lives: a conference microsite
0mentions of it on the homepage, nav, or Smart Search product page

That's not an incremental "better search" pitch, it's an argument about what a WordPress site becomes once its real audience includes AI agents. It's the right opening because it's already true, already WP Engine's own thinking, and already more sophisticated than what any competitor in this set has published.

The proof WP Engine already has and isn't using loudly enough

WP Engine opened its MCP (Model Context Protocol) Server to the whole WordPress ecosystem, not just its own customers, per the August 11, 2026 press release. That means sites hosted on competitors' platforms can use WP Engine's AI infrastructure. No other host in this competitive set has shipped anything comparable. Right now that fact lives in a blog post. It should be the headline evidence for the entire category claim.

The company with the strongest evidence is making the weakest claim, and the company with the weakest evidence is making the strongest one.

The ownership reframe

WP Engine's own agentic-web content is reactive by word choice: "Future-Proofing Content: Plugging WordPress Into the Agentic Web," "Why WordPress Needs to Plug Into the Agentic Web," "Get Your WordPress Site Ready for a World of AI Agents." Every headline frames WordPress as reacting to a shift happening to it. Automattic didn't hedge, "Operating System of the Agentic Web" is a noun-claim of ownership, backed only by WordPress's existing scale stats (43% of all websites, 60%+ CMS market share). WP Engine doesn't need to out-hedge Automattic. It has the stronger evidence to make the stronger claim, and currently isn't.

Where this belongs

Not a new keynote, and not another blog post. This needs to be the connective layer that's currently missing between the AI Toolkit docs (developer-facing) and the Smart Search AI product page (feature-facing, currently pitching "even more powerful" search with zero mention of agents, MCP, or the agentic web). A standalone pillar narrative, linked from the homepage, that makes the Four-Problem Framework the front door instead of a keynote artifact.

Why I structured it this way

Capability without category ownership

I picked the comparison table over a straight critique because the gap here isn't that WP Engine lacks a category argument, it's that the argument exists, is genuinely stronger than its competitors', and is locked in the wrong place. That's a sharper, more specific problem than "your messaging needs work," and it's the exact skill this role hires for: taking ambiguous, fragmented technical reality and defining the category narrative that makes it cohere.

I anchored on Automattic and Hostinger specifically, not a generic competitive set, because both are live, dated, public claims made in the same window WP Engine's own infrastructure shipped. A brief built on real, current competitive moves is defensible in a way a hypothetical "here's what you're missing" deck isn't.
