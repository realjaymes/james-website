A category teardown for Arcanna

Arcanna's Three Definitions

James Praise · Product Marketing

Live research on arcanna.ai's own pages, the four named competitors' own sites, and live search results, run September 2026. This is a public-surface read only, I have no access to Arcanna's internal positioning debate, so it's a starting question, not a verdict.

The teardown

What Arcanna calls itself

Arcanna's own pages don't agree with each other on the category name. Three surfaces, three different leads:

PageWhat it leads with
Homepage"Trustworthy AI for Security Operations," with the body copy calling Arcanna an "AI Platform for SOC"
Manifesto"Own the Judgment. Govern the Verdict.," anchored on "the Decision Layer for your SOC"
Careers page (this job posting)the only page that calls Arcanna "the category-defining Decision Intelligence platform for security operations"

The About page comes closest to bridging the two: it describes Arcanna's product as "a decision-intelligence layer, a way to encode analyst judgment into governed models," but that line sits in body copy, not a headline, and "AI Platform for SOC" is still the page's primary self-description. So of the four pages that make a category claim, only one uses "Decision Intelligence" as the actual headline, and it's the careers page, the one page a prospective employee reads, not a prospective buyer.

A buyer who reads the homepage, then the manifesto, then a sales deck built from either, has no reason to ever hear the words "Decision Intelligence" said about Arcanna.

What the named competitors call themselves

None of the four competitors named in this job posting use "Decision Intelligence" either. Each one has picked a different lane, and all four have converged on "agentic" language faster than Arcanna has converged on its own:

CompanyWhat it leads with
Tines"You told everyone to use AI. Now give them a secure place to do it." Tines calls itself a governance layer for "AI agents, apps and automation," and uses "AI SOC" only when addressing security teams specifically. It's the broadest of the four, security is one use case among several.
Torq"Every alert needs attention. Not every alert needs a human." Torq calls itself "The AI SOC Platform," built around "Agentic SecOps" and its own named "HyperAgents" product line. Torq has committed hardest to the word "agentic" of the four.
Swimlane"Agentic AI automation for every security function." Swimlane has visibly dropped "SOAR" from its own homepage language in favor of "Agentic AI automation," built around its "Turbine" platform.
D3 Security"The Accountable Agentic SOC Platform," built around its "Morpheus" reasoning engine. D3 explicitly distances itself from both traditional SOAR and multi-agent systems, the closest positioning analog to Arcanna's own anti-black-box stance, just framed through "accountable" rather than "governed" or "owned."

Worth keeping straight for any battlecard: Swimlane is not only a competitor here. Arcanna's own Swimlane integration page and Swimlane's own joint solution brief both describe Arcanna as sitting on top of Swimlane's SOAR playbooks, gating them with analyst-taught decisions. So Swimlane is simultaneously the platform Arcanna integrates with and, on its own homepage, a direct "agentic AI automation" competitor. A category definition that flattens that into simple us-versus-them would be wrong on a partner relationship that's actually live.

What AI search returns today

I ran two different searches to see how the category actually surfaces. Asked directly by name, "Arcanna.ai decision intelligence platform," search results confidently describe Arcanna as "a decision-intelligence platform built for security operations teams and MSSPs," pulling from Arcanna's own funding press release and its PeerSpot listing, which tracks Arcanna's mindshare inside the "AI-SOC category," not a "Decision Intelligence" category, at 2.3%.

Asked the generic question a buyer would actually type, "decision intelligence SOC security operations platform," Arcanna doesn't appear in the results at all. What surfaces instead: Seceon's SOC Automation Platform, Torq's own "AI SOC, Explained" post, Anomali (which markets its own "Security Decision Engine"), and Palo Alto Networks' AI SOC tools comparison.

Arcanna owns the story when someone already knows its name. Nobody owns the term "Decision Intelligence" yet when someone doesn't.

That's the actual opportunity, not a problem to apologize for. The generic query is open. No competitor has claimed "Decision Intelligence" as its own headline either, which means the term is winnable, but only for whoever anchors it hard enough, consistently enough, and publicly enough that search and AI answer engines start attaching it to one name.

What I'd test first

One sentence, said out loud, approved before it's written anywhereNot a rewrite of the manifesto's "Own the Judgment. Govern the Verdict." line, that's a strong tagline and should stay. The gap is one plain definition underneath it: something like "Decision Intelligence is the governance layer that makes an AI-driven SOC decision explainable and auditable, not just fast," tested on a real sales call for whether a CISO repeats it back unprompted.
Homepage hero first, not the about pageThe homepage is the page a buyer actually lands on. Right now "Decision Intelligence" only appears on the careers page, the one surface a buyer never reads. I'd move the tested definition into the homepage subhead first, then the manifesto and about page, so all three finally agree.
Feed the generic query, not just the branded oneA dedicated, plainly-titled "What is Decision Intelligence" page, linked from the main nav and cited consistently across the blog and solution pages, is what search and AI answer engines need to start attaching the term to Arcanna specifically instead of returning Seceon, Torq and Anomali for the generic question.
Same line in the deck and the RSA/Black Hat talk abstractOnce the definition is tested and approved, it belongs verbatim in the opening line of the sales deck and any conference talk abstract, so the phrase compounds in the room, not just on the site.

Why I structured it this way

Checking before claiming

The original hypothesis, drafted from the job posting alone before I opened a single Arcanna page, was that "Decision Intelligence" simply wasn't defined anywhere. That turned out to be wrong once checked: the About page does use decision-intelligence language, just in body copy, not a headline, and not anywhere a buyer is likely to land first. I kept that correction in this document instead of quietly rewriting the hypothesis to look right in hindsight.

I also didn't write a blind "here's the definition Arcanna should use" line, on purpose. I don't have access to whatever internal debate already exists about the name, whether it's settled, contested, or still being tested against alternatives, so a confident definition written cold risks being wrong about something the team already knows. What I can show from the outside is where the language actually disagrees with itself today, and how a security buyer's search behavior treats the term right now. The actual definition is a first-week conversation, not something I'd hand over pre-written.
