Account intelligence for capital construction

A signal map that tells InEight's reps which capital project accounts to call this month

James Praise · GTM engineering

The signal map

Intro

The role asks for account intelligence models that move into production, and your post talks about Clay workflows that "help us find and win the right accounts." So I built a first version from public data: the signals that suggest a contractor or owner needs project controls software soon, a score, and eight real accounts scored with sources.

Your market is a few thousand large accounts, not millions of small ones. A good quarter comes from reaching the right contractor in the month it wins a big project, before a competitor does.

The signals that matter

Each signal is public, dated and checkable, so a rep can open the source before the call. I ranked them by how close they sit to a software decision.

The score

The score adds three parts out of 100. Fit says whether the account could ever buy. Timing says whether now is the moment. Engagement says whether they already know InEight.

PartPoints
Fit (up to 40)Backlog or capital plan over $5B: 30. $1B to $5B: 20. Under $1B: 10. In an industry and region InEight serves: +10
Timing (up to 40, last 12 months)Procurement notice: 40. Project award or program approval: 20. Acquisition: 10. New leader: 10. Project controls hiring: 10. Public statement: 5
Engagement (up to 20, InEight's own data)Demo or pricing page visit by a known account: 10. Webinar or content download: 5. Email engagement: 5

Tiers. Tier 1 is 60 or more, Tier 2 is 40 to 59 and Tier 3 is under 40. Any procurement notice goes straight to Tier 1. Two separate signals within 30 days move an account up one tier, because a cluster of news means things are moving.

How it runs in Clay and Salesforce

  1. Accounts in. The target list comes from Salesforce. Customers and open opportunities are removed by a lookup before anything else runs.
  2. Fit. A waterfall of company data providers fills size, industry and region, with backlog or capital plan taken from filings where public.
  3. Timing. One AI research column per signal type. Each must return a date, a one-line fact and the source address, or "none." A signal with no source is dropped.
  4. Score and tier. A formula column applies the points and the 30-day rule.
  5. Out to Salesforce. Five account fields: score, tier, latest signal, its date and its source link. When an account moves up a tier, the owner gets a task with the source attached.
  6. Refresh. The table reruns weekly. Signals older than 12 months stop counting.

The source link on every signal is the part I would not skip. A rep trusts a score they can check in ten seconds, and ignores one they can't.

The same score on inbound

Your demo form asks for name, work email, company, phone and country, and nothing about the project. So the speed-to-lead flow should enrich the company the moment the form arrives and look up its tier. A Tier 1 account goes to its named owner within minutes. A new company gets scored on the spot and routed by tier.

How I'd test it

  • Signal precision. Each week someone checks 25 random signals against their sources. Precision is the share where the source says what the column says. I'd ship at 90% and roll back to the last prompt version if it falls under 80% two weeks running. Each prompt version is logged with its date.
  • Backtest before launch. Run the model on the last four quarters. If won accounts didn't show a timing signal in the six months before the opportunity opened more often than other accounts, the weights change before any rep sees a score.
  • Rep feedback. A one-click "useful" or "not useful" on each tier-change task, reviewed monthly with Sales Ops.

What I could not see

I had no access to your Salesforce data, your customer list or your web traffic, so engagement is zero for every account and some of these companies may already be customers or open deals. Job postings for project controls roles blocked automated reading, so no hiring signal is scored here; in Clay that signal would come from a job-posting source. The point values are a starting point to replace with what the backtest shows.
