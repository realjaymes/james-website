A plan for the Linux Foundation

From One Summit to 90 Days: A Promotion Plan for LF Energy Projects

James Praise · Product Marketing Operator

Built only from LF Energy's public pages: the LF Energy homepage, the 2025 Summit Europe recap, the 2026 agenda and session schedule, and the new members and projects announcement. The numbers are the ones LF Energy has published, and the calendar is a proposal for a Project Director to correct. The main Berlin conference ended on September 16, 2026, so I would join that campaign at week 6, and the first five weeks are the runbook for the next event.

The plan

What the summits give you

The 2025 Summit Europe recap reports 365 attendees, a nearly 50% increase over the year before, with over 100 sessions and about a dozen projects running live demos. It also says attendees told LF Energy the summit had already brought their projects new contributors, potential end users and ideas. Videos, photos and slides were published afterwards.

The 2026 event was held in Berlin on September 15 and 16. Its 2026 agenda lists presenters from RTE, TenneT, Alliander, Elia, E.ON, 50Hertz, Hydro-Québec, Hitachi, Microsoft and Red Hat, including a joint Alliander and SOPTIM session titled “Bringing the Industry Together Through LF Energy: A Power Grid Model Story” on September 16, an OpenSTEF 4 launch session on September 15, and a SEAPATH meetup on September 17. The new members and projects announcement welcomed Coreso as a general member on the first day of the summit, and LF Energy publishes a TenneT case study on how TenneT cut grid security calculations from minutes to seconds in five months on PowSyBl.

That is a lot of material, spread across more than 35 projects that each have their own audience. The job of the 90 days is to give the projects that ran sessions their own runs of content instead of one shared recap, starting with the three that have the next milestones.

Week 5: TenneT on PowSyBl, as a carousel

The carousel is for engineers and managers at transmission system operators and regional coordination centres who are weighing PowSyBl. It tells the TenneT case study in six slides.

Select any slide to open it full size. The carousel opens as a set you can swipe or step through with the arrows.

LinkedIn post with the carousel

TenneT's grid security calculations used to take minutes. They now take seconds.

TenneT runs the high-voltage grid in the Netherlands and a large part of Germany. Its team built ReFlow, an in-house orchestrator that runs many calculations in parallel on PowSyBl, hosted by LF Energy. ReFlow starts calculation pods for each job and shuts them down when the job finishes.

The result is at least a tenfold speedup across standard grid security processes. The first production release took about five months, from October 2025 to March 2026, with no proprietary licensing.

“The open source model allowed us to adopt a state-of-the-art solution without a lengthy procurement process,” says Hugo Pfister, Manager Grid Security Applications at TenneT Netherlands.

RTE, BalticRCC and Coreso also run PowSyBl in production. Read the full case study: https://lfenergy.org/grid-security-at-scale-tennet-powsybl/

What the agents do

This is the workflow I already run for my own marketing, aimed at LF Energy. Four steps, with a person approving before anything is published.

First, an agent reads the session schedule and each project's public pages and matches every session to a project. Second, it drafts the week's pieces in a project-specific voice from a short style sheet that the Project Director approves once. Third, a checking agent compares every number, name and date in each draft with its source page and flags anything it cannot match. Fourth, approved drafts are queued in HubSpot for email and in Sprout Social for social, and a person presses publish.

I run this pattern every week on the Marketing In Action newsletter, where Claude Code skills turn one approved issue into Substack, X Article and LinkedIn newsletter drafts, I review each one before it publishes, and a daily job records every live post in one registry. My content calendar works the same way, with scheduled agents drafting candidate posts and publishing only the ones I approve. I built it for a B2B team at FunnelEnvy, where one approved asset became more than ten distribution outputs with a review step before anything went live. The method is described on the Marketing Agent Skills repository I publish, with 34 of the 81 skills I use.

What I would report

One page per project, every two weeks, for the Project Director and the sponsors. Reach and clicks per piece. Email sign-ups and replies. Visits to the repository and the documentation from campaign links. New issues and first-time contributors in the weeks after each contributor call. For the event itself, session video views and sign-ups to the next event, compared with the previous cycle.

The recap's own claim, that the summit brought projects new contributors, is the number to protect. Tracking first-time contributors per project before and after the plan runs is how that claim becomes a trend line.

Why I structured it this way

One run per project

The posting asks for content calendars mapped to campaign milestones and for event promotion that maximizes return. A summit is the biggest milestone a project community has all year, and the people who attend are the most likely to contribute. I chose to work from the sessions because they are public, they already belong to a project, and they come with speakers who can be interviewed. I also kept the first five weeks general because the same runbook has to work for the next event, not only the one that just ended.

Day-one questions

I would start by learning which three projects have the next milestones and who directs each one. I would learn which channels each community already reads, and which of those the project owns. I would also ask to see today's sponsor report, so the first one I send matches it. Those answers would change the calendar above more than anything else I could research from the outside.
