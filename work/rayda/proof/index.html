The experiment system, the full cadence and the 90 day plan

How tests are run and decided

This is my own growth experimentation system, applied to Rayda. It makes sure every channel above is tested the same way, on the same unit economics, and that what is learned stays in the company when people leave. I built the ICEEE experimentation framework with tracking templates during my work at FunnelEnvy.

The rule above everything: no test runs without a written hypothesis, a success measure and a kill rule. The success measure is cost per MQL set against what a customer is worth. Clicks, impressions, registrations and badge scans are tracked and never reported as results.

Step 1 to 3: write it, score it, log it

Each test is written in eight parts: the observation that sparked it, the objective, a hypothesis in the form "by doing X we believe Y will happen, and if we are right we expect Z" with a number in Z, the design with its control and length, the open questions, the success criteria, the measurement and the results written within five working days.

Each test is then scored. Impact and Confidence are scored from 1 to 10 and 1 to 5, and each of the three efforts from 1 (under half a day) to 5 (five to ten days). Engineering effort counts double because it is the scarcest resource, and Rayda has no marketing engineer. Anything at 12 or above is a candidate for the next cycle. For Rayda I add two columns, the dollar cost to test and a note on learning value, because a channel test costs money where a page test does not, and when two scores tie the one that teaches more goes first.

Every test gets one row in the experiment log, kept in Notion or whatever sheet the team already opens. The fields are an ID, channel, one named owner, the observation, objective and hypothesis, the ICEEE scores, planned and actual cost, start, end and fixed read dates, audience and size, control and test, the success measure and kill rule written before launch and never edited, the primary result (MQLs, cost per MQL, booked calls), the downstream result (held calls, opportunities, customers, filled in late because B2B lags), the decision and a learning a stranger could act on.

Step 4 to 6: write it up, build the knowledge base, improve the system

Within five working days of the read date the owner writes one page: what was tested, the numbers against the kill rule, the decision and the next test it suggests. An inconclusive result says how many more MQLs it would have taken to read it, so the mistake does not repeat.

On top of the log sits a knowledge base with four parts: one page per channel with its best cost per MQL, audience and message; short playbooks for channels that graduate; a library of which hooks, offers and proof points won with which buyer and company size; and a kill list of what failed and why, so nobody reruns it blind.

A channel that meets its target in two separate tests graduates to a standing monthly budget. A test that meets its kill rule stops on the read date, and the money is not extended to see if it turns around. Scores are updated every month, which is how the offline ideas and the online channels compete on equal terms. Content-led inbound is the control, since it is the channel known to work, and a new channel has to beat or complement it on cost per qualified call. Attribution is fixed first: every demo form asks "how did you hear about us" in free text, and I check it against the CRM source field.

Statistical honesty at Rayda's volume. Rayda books 10 to 15 calls a month, which is too little traffic for page-level A/B tests. The system runs channel-level bets with a spending cap and a kill rule, and reads results over 4 to 8 weeks. Page changes ship as improvements and are checked before and after.
