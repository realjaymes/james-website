A plan for Aiven

A Postgres exit play

James Praise · Growth, go-to-market and AI systems

I built this from public sources only, checked on 10 November 2026. I have seen none of your sales data, so demand for this play is a hypothesis. The first step below tests it before anything gets built.

When IBM bought Confluent, you gave Kafka buyers a reason and a route to move. Postgres went through the same kind of ownership change in 2025. I propose the same play for Postgres, split into a self-serve campaign and a sales-led campaign.

Postgres today

Databricks agreed to buy Neon in May 2025, and Neon's pricing page now reads "from Databricks". Snowflake bought Crunchy Data a few weeks later.

A buyer who chose an independent Postgres vendor now has a vendor inside a data platform company. Some of those buyers will be happy about it. Others will want to keep their database separate from their analytics vendor, and those are the people this play is for.

Your Postgres page describes the product well and names no rival. Your 66 Postgres ads that I sampled sell the product on its features. None of what I found speaks to someone reconsidering a vendor.

Appendix: measures and stop rules

Main measure. Qualified pipeline with a Postgres exit source, split between the self-serve and sales-led campaigns. I would read it against the Confluent play at the same age, since you already hold those numbers.

Early signals. For the developer campaign, I would watch comparison-page visit to signup and signup to first running service. For the regulated campaign, I would watch message ad replies and migration reviews booked.

Before building. If sales notes and the 10 conversations show little interest in leaving Neon or Crunchy Data, the play moves to Amazon RDS.

After launch. I would stop paid spend on either audience if its cost per qualified conversation is clearly worse than the Kafka play's at the same age, and keep the pages live for search.

Appendix: how I checked

The ad counts come from LinkedIn's Ad Library, searching advertiser "aiven". LinkedIn matches names loosely, so a count can include a similarly named advertiser. I read the first results of each search and they were yours.
