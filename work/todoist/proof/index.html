A brief for Todoist

A Ramble Attribution & Measurement Plan

James Praise · Marketing and GTM Operator

This is a proposed plan, not an audit of Todoist's current tracking setup. Todoist's internal instrumentation isn't publicly visible, so nothing here claims to know what's currently missing. It proposes how I'd build the measurement layer for a Ramble-specific acquisition push, the same discipline behind my own paid-acquisition measurement system for Marketing In Action, applied to this feature specifically.

The plan

The actual question

A paid campaign built around Ramble can generate clicks and app installs easily. The harder question, the one that actually separates a loop from a campaign, is whether someone who clicked a Ramble ad tries Ramble, and whether trying it is what pushed them to upgrade to a paid plan. Click and install numbers from Google, Meta, or TikTok can't answer that on their own, they report what happened on their platform, not what happened inside the product afterward.

Without that link, a Ramble-specific campaign either gets credited for upgrades it didn't cause, people who would have upgraded anyway, or doesn't get credited for upgrades it did cause, someone clicks, doesn't convert same-day, uses Ramble two weeks later, then upgrades. Both failure modes lead to the same bad outcome: spending against the wrong signal.

The tracking plan

The last row is the one that answers the question. The first five are just the ingredients.

Where platform numbers are likely to mislead

Multi-touch attribution windowsIf someone clicks a Ramble ad on their phone, doesn't convert, then upgrades two weeks later on desktop after a brand search, most ad platforms will still credit that upgrade to the Ramble campaign inside their own attribution window. Requiring ramble_first_use, actual feature usage, sidesteps this.
View-through inflationAd platforms count impressions someone never consciously registered as influencing a conversion. A campaign that looks efficient on-platform can look completely different once judged against ramble_first_use as the bar instead of a view-through window.
Existing users mistaken for new acquisitionIf the landing page doesn't check whether the click came from an existing logged-in user before triggering the attributed-signup logic, some fraction of "new signups" will be existing users opening a new tab, inflating the apparent acquisition number for a feature they already had access to.

How I'd build it

The same stack I already run for my own business: Google Tag Manager for the client-side events, a first-party server-side layer for the events that need to survive ad blockers and iOS tracking restrictions, the signup and upgrade events specifically, and GA4 or BigQuery as the place the joined, attributed view lives, reconciled against the real billing system rather than trusting any single platform's own dashboard.

Two weeks of setup before the campaign goes live: week one wires the events and confirms ramble_first_use is either already logged or gets added, week two runs a small test batch of traffic through the full funnel to confirm the join logic in upgrade_attributed_ramble works before real budget follows it.

Why I built it this way

A proposal, not an audit

Todoist's internal tracking setup isn't publicly visible, so this plan doesn't claim to know what's currently missing or broken. What's stated directly is the requirement for data rigor across GA4, Tag Manager, and BigQuery. This plan answers that concretely, with the events, the join logic, and the failure modes a Ramble-specific campaign would need to watch for, rather than a general statement about caring about measurement.

The three failure modes aren't hypothetical. They're the same problem, an ad platform's own number quietly diverging from what happened, that caught a real cross-attribution error in my own Meta reporting for Marketing In Action. The fix there was the same fix proposed here: reconcile against the system that actually took the payment, not the system that served the ad.
