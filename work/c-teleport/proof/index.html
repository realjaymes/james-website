A brief for C Teleport

Energy & Offshore Demand-Gen Gap Brief

James Praise · Demand Generation & GTM Engineer

Built entirely from C Teleport's own public surfaces: cteleport.com's solutions pages, case studies, blog archive, and events page, plus two live buyer-intent search checks.

The brief

Three verticals, three maturity levels

Marine, Aircrew, and Energy & Offshore each have their own solutions page and negotiated fare product, equal product-level investment. Content investment isn't equal. Of 4 case studies, 3 are Marine and 1 (Dutch Offshore, published Feb 2026) is a hybrid maritime/offshore account, the company's first tentative step into this vertical. Of 15 blog posts since 2018, exactly 1 mentions offshore, filed under the "maritime" category rather than its own vertical.

1 of 4case studies touch Energy & Offshore, published this year
1 of 15blog posts since 2018 mention offshore, filed under "maritime"
0appearances on either buyer-intent search checked

What the search check found

Two searches a demand gen engineer would treat as core queries for this ICP: "offshore energy workforce travel management software" (results: IBS Software, BCD Travel, ATPI, Onboard Tracker) and "wind farm technician crew travel booking software" (results: OpusFlow, RosterElf, Onboard Tracker, LodgeLink, DSPTCH). C Teleport did not appear in either, despite having a dedicated Energy & Offshore solutions page and fare structure.

C Teleport is paying to exhibit at OEEC 2025, RAI Amsterdam, the exact vertical it doesn't show up for on Google.

The self-serve mechanic

What existsWhat's missing
"Up to 45%" (marine) and "up to 75%" (energy) efficiency claims, already publishedAny self-serve mechanism to turn those figures into a personalized estimate
Uniform "book a demo" CTA across the entire siteA soft top-of-funnel step between an OEEC booth visit or a cold organic hit and a sales-ready demo request

A lightweight, self-serve energy/offshore crew-travel savings calculator, built directly on the published efficiency figures, sized as an automation deliverable, not just a content brief, is the "engineered growth systems" half of this role, not just messaging judgment. It's also the piece that turns the case-study and content gaps above into an actual conversion path, rather than three separate fixes with no connecting mechanism.

Why I structured it this way

Systems, not just copy

I built two components, a content/SEO gap brief and a drafted conversion mechanic, because the role is titled Demand Generation Engineer, not Content Marketer. A gap brief alone would be a messaging critique. Pairing it with a buildable, automated conversion mechanic tied to real published numbers demonstrates the actual skill this role hires for: not just noticing what's missing, but engineering the system that closes it.
