Built for Klarent

When a quality assurance lead asks an AI assistant for a tool, the answer does not name Klarent

James Praise · Product Marketing, Search and AI Answer Visibility

I ran this audit on 16 October 2026 for Asheem Panakkat and the Klarent team. It is one run, and AI answers change between runs, so read the numbers as a snapshot. The method repeats every week. Every number on this page comes from that run, and I saved every answer and every source. The method is the open source audit I built for Arcessio: github.com/realjaymes/ai-answer-visibility-audit.

The result

The short version

The posting asks the marketing hire to make sure Klarent is "visible and accurately represented" in ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews. So I measured it. I wrote 30 questions that a quality assurance (QA) lead or engineering lead would type, grounded in Google autocomplete, and ran each one through ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google. That gave me 117 AI answers and 30 Google results pages.

0 of 71AI answers to the 18 questions without the name Klarent that named Klarent
0 of 18of those questions where klarent.ai reached Google's top 20 results
40 of 46answers that named Klarent when the question contained the name

Twelve of the 30 questions contain the name Klarent or fore ai, such as "Klarent vs mabl." The other 18 are what a buyer types before knowing Klarent exists, such as "autonomous QA agent," "best AI testing tools 2026" and "alternatives to testRigor." On those 18, the four AI engines gave 71 answers (Google showed no AI Overview for one of them), and none named Klarent or cited klarent.ai.

Once someone types the name, the engines find the product. They named Klarent in 40 of 46 answers and cited klarent.ai in 27 of them. A buyer who is still choosing hears about mabl, QA Wolf, Tricentis, Katalon and testRigor instead. The work sits in the 18 questions, because those buyers have not heard of Klarent yet.

The method

How I ran it

Step 1Write the questions30 questions in four groups: 8 about the problem, 7 about the category, 10 that compare vendors and 5 name checks. Wording comes from Google autocomplete where it had material.
Step 2Ask four enginesChatGPT through Codex, Perplexity, Gemini and Google AI Overviews, plus Google's top 20 results. Every answer, source and search is saved.
Step 3Check the siteAll 219 pages in the klarent.ai sitemap, fetched the way an AI crawler fetches them, plus robots.txt, llms.txt and structured data.
Step 4Score and rankMention rate per engine, citation share by domain, who owns each question, then a ranked fix list.

Two engines did not run the way I planned, and you should know that before you trust the numbers. My OpenAI account had no credit today, so the ChatGPT answers come from the Codex command line tool, which runs an OpenAI model (gpt-5.6-sol) with live web search. It stands in for the ChatGPT app, and I could not set its location to the United States. Gemini decides for itself when to search the web. It searched for 17 of the 30 questions and for 5 of the 18 without the name, so 13 of its 18 answers to those questions come from the model's memory. Perplexity and Gemini ran through DataForSEO's service. Google results and AI Overviews are for the United States, in English, on desktop.

What the answers say about determinism and flaky tests

Flaky tests and determinism are Klarent's argument, so I recorded what each answer says about them. Of the 71 answers, 34 mention flaky tests and 28 mention determinism. When an answer ties a vendor to the word deterministic, it names QA Wolf in four answers and Applitools in two. It never names Klarent. The ChatGPT answer to "AI software testing agent" recommends QA Wolf for deterministic automation, and the answer to "deterministic AI test automation" points to Applitools and a "Deterministic Language Model."

When the name is typed, the engines split. Gemini ties Klarent to determinism in 10 of its 12 answers, and Google AI Overviews do it in 2 of 8. The ChatGPT route and Perplexity do it in none of their 20. They describe Klarent as no code, plain English and self healing, the same words mabl, testRigor and Testsigma use. I did not test why the engines differ. The homepage, the about page and the Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) page never use the word, and the funding announcement does.

The plan

Five fixes, ranked by how likely they are to earn citations

1. A guide to flaky tests that carries Asheem's mathPublish "What causes flaky tests, and how to stop them (2026)." Open with the math: a suite of 100 tests that are each right 99% of the time fails at least once in about 63% of runs. Show how a deterministic engine removes the cause, add FAQPage markup, and link the guide from the homepage, llms.txt and the comparison pages.Why first: 34 of the 71 answers mention flaky tests, the answers to the eight problem questions cite pytest, TestRail, Sauce Labs, the Google Testing Blog and Telerik, and no page on klarent.ai is about flaky tests. The 99% math is not on any blog, glossary or author page.
2. A page that defines deterministic AI test automationTitle it with the phrase buyers use. Say in the first paragraph that AI writes the tests and a fixed engine runs them, and show the exported test code. Add the word to the homepage, the FAQ, the about page and the opening lines of llms.txt, where it appears zero times today.Why second: the question "deterministic AI test automation" returned Applitools and general frameworks and never Klarent. QA Wolf is tied to the word in four other answers. Gemini repeats Klarent's claim when the name is typed, and the other engines do not.
3. Alternatives pages for the five vendors buyers compare againstPublish "testRigor alternatives," "mabl alternatives," "Tricentis Tosca alternatives," "Katalon alternatives" and "Testsigma alternatives," each with an honest comparison table and a dated stamp.Why third: none of the 12 answers to the three alternatives questions named Klarent. The most cited domains there were g2.com, testsigma.com and reddit.com. Katalon and Testsigma have no Klarent page at all, and the answers named them in 17 and 10 of 71 answers.
4. One ranked list of autonomous QA tools for 2026Publish "Best autonomous QA and AI testing tools in 2026," comparing 12 tools on how they work: who writes the test, what runs it, who reviews it and whether the code can be exported. Rank Klarent honestly inside the field.Why fourth: six ranked lists with 2026 in the title were each cited in 3 answers, and QA Wolf published one of them on its own site.
5. Same day changes to llms.txt and FAQ schemaAdd the seven comparison pages, the glossary and the author index to llms.txt. Rewrite its opening summary to lead with determinism. Add FAQPage markup to the 11 blog posts that lack it, starting with the founder posts.This ships in a day. I have not tested that either change earns a citation, so I rank it last and would measure it on the next run.

What I would do next is put these 30 questions on a weekly run, report the share of the 18 questions where Klarent is named, and tie each fix to a change in that number.

Rename cleanup checklist

Klarent's own pages disagree about the old name, and the AI answers repeat the disagreement. This is the order I would work through it.

1. Decide what the company is called, then say it one wayThe August post "Introducing Klarent by fore ai" says "our company remains fore ai." The FAQ says "fore ai is the company's former name." The privacy policy and terms still name fore ai AG and link to www.foreai.co. If the legal entity is still fore ai AG, say "fore ai AG, trading as Klarent" once in the legal pages and nowhere else.
2. Rewrite the FAQ and homepage answers that treat fore ai as the companyThe answer that begins "Klarent is fore ai's autonomous" and the phrases "Contact the fore ai team" and "the fore ai Trust Center" sit in both the FAQ and the homepage questions, and in their structured data. That is 15 mentions on the FAQ page and 4 on the homepage. Keep the one answer to "Is Klarent the same as fore ai?"
3. Keep the alternate name and fix the linksThe Organization data names Klarent and lists fore ai as an alternate name. I would keep that, because it ties old mentions to the new name. Rename the LinkedIn company page and update its link, which still uses the slug fore-ai, and update the trust center link (foreai.eu.trust.site). Every one of the 219 pages carries at least one link or address with the old name.
4. Turn foreai.co into a redirectThe old domain shows a note that it will redirect to klarent.ai in 15 seconds, stays open to indexing and names itself as canonical. Deep links such as foreai.co/products/test-management/ return a 404, yet AI answers cited that address three times. AI answers cited foreai.co 9 times across the 30 questions. Send every path to its klarent.ai equivalent with a server redirect, or at minimum point the canonical at klarent.ai. The documentation subdomain already does the second.
5. Update the profiles that still use the old nameThese appeared in Google on 16 October: the Microsoft Marketplace listing (its address still contains fore-ai, and 10 answers cited it), the GitHub organization named fore ai, startup.ch, joinup.ch, Dealroom and PeerSpot.
6. Separate Klarent from Klarent HospitalityGoogle's first four results for "Klarent" are about Klarent Hospitality, and klarent.ai appears fifth with its documentation page. The homepage does not appear in the top 20. Five of the ten autocomplete suggestions for "klarent" are about the hotel company, and Gemini answered the bare name with two companies. Put "Klarent" first in the homepage title, repeat one phrase such as "Klarent autonomous QA" in titles and profile descriptions, and link the Crunchbase, LinkedIn and Microsoft Marketplace profiles from the Organization data.
7. Run the 12 name questions againThe ChatGPT answers called Klarent a product from or by fore ai in 5 of the 9 answers that named it. Re-run those questions after the changes above and compare.

Method and limits

I wrote the questions and grounded them in Google autocomplete where it had material. Autocomplete returned no extra suggestions for "test automation maintenance," "mabl alternatives," "testRigor alternatives" or "deterministic test automation," so those questions use my wording. Autocomplete for "klarent" and "fore ai" is where I found the name collisions.

Twelve of the 30 questions contain Klarent or fore ai, so the headline numbers use the 18 that do not. A citation count of one means an answer cited the domain at least once. The ChatGPT route lists the sources the model says it used. Perplexity, Gemini and Google list the sources attached to the answer.

I ran each question once. AI answers vary between runs, and a weekly rerun turns this snapshot into a trend. I did not test whether any fix earns a citation, what the ChatGPT app itself would say, or other countries, languages and engines such as Copilot and Claude.
