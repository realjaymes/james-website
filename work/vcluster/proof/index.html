The audit

Eight questions a GPU cloud buyer might ask

The posting says the job starts with "an audit of where we stand" in search and AI answers. This is the outside half of that audit. I wrote eight prompts in the words a platform lead at an AI cloud, a telco or an enterprise AI team might use. For each answer, I recorded whether it named vCluster as a product and whether it cited vcluster.com as a source. Named means the answer recommended vCluster. Cited in six answers, named in two

vcluster.com was a source in six of the eight answers. vCluster was named as a product in only two of them. That means your pages are teaching the AI assistants how the problem works, and the assistants are then recommending someone else.

The two shortlist questions, the ones that ask for a list of vendors, matter most. That is where a buyer builds a vendor list. Prompt 3, "which companies sell software to run an AI factory or GPU cloud," named NVIDIA, CoreWeave, Lambda and Nebius. CoreWeave and Nebius are on your own customers page. Prompt 7, about a telco's sovereign AI cloud, named Rafay.

vCluster explains the problem well enough to be cited. It isn't yet the name that comes back when a buyer asks who to call.

The answers that left vCluster out leaned on three kinds of source: NVIDIA's developer site, third-party tool lists, and vendors who answer one question by name, such as Lago for GPU billing. Those are the places to win first.

The best sovereign story isn't on the sovereign page

On 21 October 2026, Deutsche Telekom put NVIDIA DSX OS into production on its Industrial AI Cloud with vCluster, and the case study reports 35 times faster customer onboarding. That is the strongest sovereign AI proof in the market.

Your sovereign clouds page, "Launch a Sovereign AI Cloud in Days," doesn't mention Deutsche Telekom or DSX OS. A buyer or an AI assistant reading that page for prompt 7 never meets the proof. The fix takes one afternoon: the 35 times figure, one quote, the DSX OS detail and a link to the case study.

I'd expect the same gap on other solution pages. I'd check each one against its best customer proof in the first two weeks.

The plan

First 90 days

Days 1 to 30: set the baseline

  • Pull organic rankings and clicks for your buyer terms from Search Console needs your data.
  • Set up an AI referral channel group in Google Analytics 4 (GA4) so visits from chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, gemini.google.com and claude.ai are counted on their own.
  • Agree a fixed panel of 30 buyer prompts with sales and product marketing, and run it across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Claude.
  • Add the Deutsche Telekom proof to the sovereign clouds page, and map every solution page to its best customer story.

Days 31 to 60: win the shortlist answers

  • Publish one page per shortlist question that answers it by name: software for AI clouds and AI factories, sovereign AI cloud software for telcos, and GPU metering and billing (vBilling).
  • Pitch a co-authored piece on DSX OS in production with Deutsche Telekom for NVIDIA's ecosystem channels, the source the sovereign answer leaned on.
  • Get vCluster listed, with accurate facts, on the third-party tool lists the answers cited.
  • Restart the monthly webinar, starting with the Deutsche Telekom story.

Days 61 to 90: build the engine

  • Run the calendar below at one anchor piece a week, each with a named author.
  • Turn the expert interviews run by Lukas Gentele, vCluster's CEO, into executive bylines.
  • Scope the annual research report on AI cloud operations, possibly with a research partner such as SemiAnalysis, which already co-hosts your events.
  • Re-run the prompt panel and report the change.
