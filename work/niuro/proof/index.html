Built for Niuro

A hiring-signal scan, what it found and what broke

James Praise · Growth, go-to-market and AI systems

I ran this on 10 November 2026 against public job feeds only. I haven't seen Niuro's customer records or Hiring OS data, so the seed list is my guess at a Niuro buyer, and the scores are a starting point to test against the accounts that already bought from you.

Carlos wrote that he wants to meet people who can show something they've built, explain why it worked and say what broke. This page does that for one small system, built for Niuro. It finds US software companies whose engineering roles are not filling, which is the problem your homepage sells against: "backend work keeps slipping and we can't ship fast enough."

What I built

Most US tech companies post jobs through an applicant tracking system (ATS) such as Greenhouse, Ashby or Lever. All three publish a public job feed in JSON that anyone can read without a login. The scan reads those feeds and does four things for each company.

  1. It keeps the engineering roles and drops sales engineers, solutions architects, recruiters and other titles that only look technical.
  2. It sorts each role by age: posted in the last 21 days, open between 60 days and a year, or open more than a year.
  3. It counts how many roles are in AI, ML, backend, platform or data work, and how many are remote.
  4. It gives the company a score and a tier, then writes a table and a JSON file.

A role open between two months and a year scores highest. That is a real opening the company has not filled, and the hiring manager feels it every week. Fresh roles score a little. The share of AI or backend roles and the share of remote roles add a little more, because those are the roles a nearshore team can fill.

The tiers are a commercial call. Tier 1 is 5 to 40 open engineering roles with at least two stuck: big enough to need outside help, small enough not to run a large recruiting team. Tier 2 is more than 40 open roles. Those companies already have recruiters, so the pitch is staffing for overflow, not a search. Tier 3 is everything else.

What broke, and how I fixed it

1. Evergreen posts looked like pain

The first version counted every engineering role open longer than 60 days as stuck. Linear came out with nine stuck roles, and one of them had been posted 2,022 days earlier. That isn't a role Linear can't fill. Many companies keep a standing post open for years to collect applicants. I added a third age bucket for roles over a year old, which scores nothing. Linear dropped from nine stuck roles to three, and 40 roles across the list moved into the evergreen bucket.

2. The title filter let the wrong people in

The first filter matched any title with "engineer", "data" or "architect" in it. It counted a Head of Sales Data Science at Gusto, a Developer Relations Engineer at Modal, a Senior Solutions Architect at Airtable and an AI deployment engineer at Writer, who works with customers. None of those are roles a nearshore engineering team fills. I added those patterns, plus director and head of titles, to the exclusion list.

3. An empty board is not a company that stopped hiring

Mercury's Ashby board and Webflow's Greenhouse board answered with zero roles. Both companies are hiring. They moved to a different board. The first version scored them zero and moved on, which would have quietly dropped two good accounts. Now an empty board is logged as a failure to fix, the same as a 404.

4. Board names have to be found, not guessed

I guessed each company's board name, and 8 of 40 returned 404 (Hebbia, Retool, Grammarly, dbt Labs, Temporal, Plaid, Anyscale and Weaviate). With an empty board counted too, that's 10 failures out of 40. In production I'd resolve each board once from the company's careers page, store it, and run a weekly health check that flags any board that starts failing.

Still open

Two weak spots remain, and I'd fix them in the first week. Writer's 15 engineering roles were all posted in the last 21 days, which looks like a mass repost. A repost resets the date, so a stuck role can look fresh. And Greenhouse feeds only say "remote" in the location text, so Vercel shows 1 remote role out of 25 when its real policy may differ. Both are fixed by storing every role with the date the scan first saw it, then counting age from that date.

How I'd run it at Niuro

  1. Start from what already bought. Score Niuro's closed hiring processes from the last year with the same signals, and find which tier and which role mix converts. Then build the seed list from lookalikes of those accounts instead of my guess.
  2. Run it weekly and keep history. Store each role with its first-seen date, so age is true even when a company reposts. A role that crosses 60 days becomes a trigger.
  3. Pick the person. The engineering manager or head of engineering for that team first, then the recruiter. Enrich through a waterfall, then check every address with a live deliverability check before it can send.
  4. Draft with an AI model, approve with a person. A large language model (LLM) drafts from the stuck role, its age and one Niuro shortlist that matches the stack. A person approves every send of a new message until the error rate is known, then reviews a sample. Every draft is checked for the right role, a true age and no invented claims.
  5. Measure what the business counts. Hiring processes started, conversion from reply to process, cost per process (data, enrichment, model and sending costs divided by processes started), and reliability (boards read, failures and bounce rate).

Your MCP launch fits the same list. The recruiters in Tier 2 accounts already work at volume, and some of them already work in Claude. They are the first people I'd show the Niuro MCP, "11M+ people inside Claude", to.

Appendix: code and data

The scan is one Python file with no outside libraries. It makes one request at a time with a short pause, so it never puts load on a job board.

  • hiring_signal_scan.py, the scan
  • niuro_seeds.txt, the 40 seed companies and their board names
  • scan-2026-11-10.json, the full output of this run

Run it with python3 hiring_signal_scan.py --seeds niuro_seeds.txt --out scan.json --md scan.md.
