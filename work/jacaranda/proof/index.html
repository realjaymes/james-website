Built for Jacaranda

Jacaranda has no hotels yet, and the AI answers do not name it. This page holds the evidence behind the plan.

James Praise · AI go-to-market systems and distribution

I built this on 16 October 2026 for Toby Hanington and the Jacaranda team. It rests on one run, and AI answers change between runs, so read the numbers as a snapshot. The hotel sample and the search check use the same method I would repeat every week. Owner and manager names appear only where a public source names them, the email addresses stay private, and no hotel has been contacted.

The result

The short version

Jacaranda is a pre-launch collection of independent boutique hotels, and its site says "Opening later this year." It names no hotel, no starting city and no launch date. A hotel collection grows by winning hotels and then filling their rooms, so I tested both. For hotels, I built a sample of 29 candidates in Cape Town and Nairobi. For guests, I ran 25 questions a traveller would type through ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google.

29boutique hotels in Cape Town and Nairobi that fit Jacaranda's own tests, each with a source
0 of 60AI answers to the 20 traveller questions that named Jacaranda
11 of 15AI answers to the 5 questions naming Jacaranda that named or cited Jacaranda Hotels Kenya

The starting position is zero on both. That is expected for a site with no hotels. It means the first work is to sign the founding hotels, fix the name, and give the AI engines pages they can quote.

Hotels

How I built the sample

Step 1Source15 Google Maps queries across the two cities returned 237 places. I dropped chains, places under 20 reviews or below a 4.3 rating, and places with no website.
Step 2ReadI fetched each shortlisted hotel's own site (home, contact, about and rooms) for room counts and emails.
Step 3ResearchThree research agents checked 45 hotels for rooms, ownership, a named owner and a sourced design reason. Anything unconfirmed stays blank.
Step 4Score and verifyI scored each hotel out of 5, kept the 29 that scored 4 or 5, and checked each email with Hunter's verifier.

The fit score gives up to 2 points for independence (1 for a small group or unconfirmed ownership), 1 for 40 rooms or fewer (the limit on Jacaranda's For hotels page), 1 for a Google rating of 4.6 or higher with 100 or more reviews, and 1 for a sourced design, art, food or heritage reason. Hotels that scored 3 or lower were left out. Jacaranda names no hotel today, so nothing needed excluding.

Where the data is weak

  • Six hotels state no room count, and the room counts for Dorp (37) and Labotessa (7) come from sources outside the hotels.
  • Jacaranda's rule is independent, and six hotels belong to small groups. Six more have ownership I could not confirm, so they need a conversation before a pitch.
  • A role inbox such as reservations@ reaches whoever answers it. For the hotels with the highest scores, I would contact the named owner on LinkedIn first.
  • One named manager comes from a 2019 article and may be out of date.
  • I left out one hotel whose reservations inbox failed verification, one whose homepage carried injected spam links, and two guest houses with no design evidence.

The name clash

Jacaranda Hotels Kenya is a separate group with three properties and a Nairobi hotel that has 126 rooms. When a question names Jacaranda, the engines mostly describe that group. Google fares better: jacaranda.africa is in its top 20 for 4 of the 5 questions, and ranks first for "Jacaranda boutique hotel collection Africa." Its AI Overview described the collection correctly in 2 of the 4 that appeared.

  • "Jacaranda boutique hotel collection Africa": ChatGPT and Perplexity said it is a Kenyan group. Gemini described both, with the collection first.
  • "Jacaranda boutique hotels Nairobi": ChatGPT and Perplexity described the Kenyan hotel, and Gemini added the collection as a second option.
  • "Jacaranda boutique hotel Cape Town": ChatGPT and Perplexity listed a Khayelitsha bed and breakfast and a wine farm, and Google's AI Overview said no such hotel exists.
  • "Jacaranda Africa Baobab Network hotel collection": ChatGPT and the AI Overview were correct, and Gemini called Baobab a "tech accelerator and venture capital platform."

Gemini's longer answers also repeat Jacaranda's guide cities (Cape Town, Marrakech, Nairobi, Lagos, Zanzibar) as if hotels had signed there, while the site names no signed hotel. The site has no llms.txt file (a guide written for AI crawlers), and only the ten country guides carry structured data. The home page, About, For hotels and Club pages carry none.

Method

Limits

  • I ran the check once, on one day. Answer text changes between runs, so a single answer is only an example, and the totals across the 20 questions carry the evidence.
  • Source categories come from a domain list. Hotel-name counts come from bold names in the answers, with a few spellings merged by hand, and a keyword match decided whether each hotel was named.
  • I did not test Instagram or TikTok search, and I did not test Marrakech or Dakar, where search is mostly in French.
  • Jacaranda has not named a launch city. I used Cape Town and Nairobi because they are the two cities in its LinkedIn launch post where people commonly search in English. If Jacaranda names another city, I would repeat the method there.
  • The hotel sample uses public sources only. Nothing has been sent to any hotel.
