A brief for ThreatModeler

Where AI answers put ThreatModeler, and the 90 days of content that would change it

James Praise · Brand, content and go-to-market

On 16 November 2026 I asked a web-searching AI model 12 questions a security buyer might ask about threat modeling, and recorded which companies each answer named. It's one run on one model, so read it as a baseline to test. The method and its limits are in the appendix.

The audit

What the answers say

When the question is about choosing a tool, ThreatModeler shows up every time. It's named in all five usable tool-choice answers, including "best threat modeling tool for an enterprise AppSec team" and "ThreatModeler vs IriusRisk". Most of those answers cite your own platform page.

When the question is about doing the work, ThreatModeler mostly disappears. It's named in one of six answers about scaling, Secure by Design, AI-generated code, Europe's Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) and the EU AI Act, adoption research and who the experts are. Those answers cite the US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA), the Open Worldwide Application Security Project (OWASP), Microsoft, Security Compass and, twice, IriusRisk.

Five of five tool-choice answers name ThreatModeler. One of six how-to and research answers does.

I'd treat that second group as the one this role exists for, because your posting asks for "the leading voice in Secure by Design". The rest of this page is about closing the gap.

You already own most of what those answers cite

The research answer leans on the 2024–25 State of Threat Modeling report from Threat Modeling Connect. That community site now shows ThreatModeler as the company powering it, and it's collecting input for the 2025–26 edition right now. The answer credits the community and never names you.

Two other answers credit IriusRisk, a name you now own. iriusrisk.com carries more than a dozen customer stories, including ABN AMRO, ClearBank and Raiffeisen Bank International. The case study library on threatmodeler.ai shows three.

The pages are there too. Your Secure by Design page names CISA's pledge, your compliance page names DORA, and your MAESTRO post on agentic AI has a named author, a date and structured FAQ data that search engines read. The answers still went to CISA, OWASP and Microsoft.

My read is that answer engines lean on sources other sites already treat as the authority on a topic. On learning questions that means standards bodies and community research, and none of these answers cited a ThreatModeler expert. If that holds across a bigger test, the plan should aim at outside citations more than at new pages of your own.

One story across three front doors

A buyer can meet the company three ways today. The homepage says "Build Securely. Without Slowing Down." The About page says "Built to secure what's next." iriusrisk.com says "Build-Safer-Faster, The AI Threat Modeling Tool", under a banner saying it's now part of ThreatModeler.

I think your About page tells the strongest version of the story: two companies that built the deepest threat modeling knowledge from different directions, combined into one platform. I'd lift that story onto the homepage and iriusrisk.com, then hold every content program to it.

The plan

The first 90 days

Month 1: the line and the baselineAgree one sentence for the merged company with you and the executive team. Then put it on all three front doors and both LinkedIn pages. Expand this run to 40 questions across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews and Claude, and repeat it weekly for a month so the baseline is stable.
Month 2: put your experts where learning answers lookYour bench includes a Chief Technology Officer, a Chief Product Officer, the founder and a research team that already publishes. I'd get them onto the sites answer engines already trust: OWASP project contributions, Threat Modeling Connect chapter talks, standards comments and named guest pieces. Each one links back to the matching ThreatModeler page.
Month 3: make the 2025–26 report the flagshipKeep the report community-run, as Threat Modeling Connect promises. Build ThreatModeler's own analysis around it: chart pages, an executive briefing and a webinar, so answers that cite the findings can name the sponsor. In the same month, bring the IriusRisk customer stories into one library.

How I'd measure it

I'd rerun the same questions every month and report one number first: ThreatModeler's share of how-to and research answers, starting at one of six. Next to it sit organic traffic to the learning pages, branded search, and share of voice against Security Compass, which publishes its own 2025 State of Threat Modeling report.

Appendix

Method and limits

I ran each question once through the Codex command-line tool with live web search, using an open-source script I wrote. The prompt asked for a three-to-five-sentence answer and a list of every company and source used. One question came back empty, which leaves 11.

Answers change by model, day and wording, so one run is a starting point. A real baseline needs more questions, several engines and repeat runs, which is month one of the plan. Every page quoted here was read live on 16 November 2026, a few months after the IriusRisk acquisition brought the two brands together.
