A homepage teardown for Trendspek

Your funding is for North America and Europe. The only country your homepage names is Australia.

James Praise · Marketing and Go-to-Market Operator

I read your homepage, your pricing page and your funding announcement the way a North American asset owner would, as someone who has never heard of Trendspek and has one tab open. Everything here comes from public pages, checked on October 14, 2026. I have not seen the product, your analytics or your sales calls, so this is a read from the outside and the limits are at the end.

1. What the money is for

On September 30, 2026 you announced A$6 million (Australian dollars) led by OIF Ventures. Your release says the money funds expansion into North America and Europe and speeds up the AI roadmap. Spatial Source reports the same use of funds, including a larger presence in both regions.

Your careers page lists two open roles, a Senior Frontend Engineer and an Enterprise Account Executive, and both are listed for Australia. No role is open for marketing or for a North American market, and none of what follows touches the work of those two hires.

So the plan is public and the hiring has started. The question for this page is what a buyer in the new regions finds when they open your homepage before anyone from your team has spoken to them.

2. What the homepage says to a North American reader

The first screen does its job. It says "Inspect, plan and monitor assets in 3D. One record for the whole lifecycle." That names the buyer's need and no country, so it works for a reader in any region and I would not change it.

The logo strip under the headline is the one place the page carries international weight. It shows Chevron, the Alberta Geological Survey, Westfield and Acciona, among others, with the Osprey Integrity quote beneath. None of it says where the work was done.

The pricing page has the same gap. It shows the entry plans as "$10k" and "$15k" a year and never says which dollar. A buyer in Alberta or Texas cannot tell whether that is Australian or American dollars, and the two are not the same price.

The page is accurate for the market you have today, and it was written by people who work in that market. It does not yet tell a new reader that the product has been used on assets like theirs.

The case-study index shows three studies on its first page: Vocus, Applus+ and NSW Ports. The Osprey Integrity and Welsh Water studies are not among them. A reader who clicks "See more results" from the homepage lands on a page that shows an Australian telecom, a global inspection firm and an Australian port.

This is the finding that matters most. You are not short of North American and European proof. You are short of a line that points to it on the page the new buyer lands on. That is a faster fix than a new case study, because the evidence already exists and the customers are already named.

4. Four changes to test first, in order

Each change is a hypothesis with the question it answers. I have ordered them by effort, smallest first, so you can run the first two in an afternoon.

1. One line under the logo strip that says where the product is used

In use on refineries in Alberta, water assets in Wales and ports in New South Wales. Test it as a single added line directly under "Trusted by the organisations that manage critical assets." Check each place with the customer before it goes live, since I am working only from public pages. It answers one question: does a reader who has never heard of Trendspek now see that it works on assets like theirs?

2. A country on every quote and logo caption

Change the Osprey Integrity attribution to read "Courtland Penk, President, Osprey Integrity (Canada)." One word, no new design work. It tells a North American reader that someone in their market is on record, which the quote alone does not.

3. Osprey Integrity and Welsh Water on the first page of the case-study index

Move both studies onto page one, add a region tag to each card, and for visitors outside Australia feature one of them on the homepage next to NSW Ports. Both studies are written already. This is an ordering decision and a tag.

4. Name the currency, and spell for the reader

Add "AUD" (Australian dollars) or "USD" (United States dollars) beside the pricing figures, or show the reader's currency by region. "Organisations" and "minimise" read as foreign to a US buyer and as normal to a UK or European one, so decide which market the homepage is for first and let that choose the spelling. This is the lowest priority of the four. It matters most if buyers reach the pricing page early.

5. How to know whether any of this is right

I do not have your analytics, so I cannot tell you how North American visitors behave today. Treat the four changes as hypotheses. There are four ways to find out, in order of how fast they pay off.

Look at demo requests by country first

If visitors from Canada and the United States already book demos at about the rate your Australian visitors do, the homepage is not the constraint and this is a small cleanup. If they book at a clearly lower rate, it is the first thing to fix. This costs nothing and takes an hour.

Run a five-second test with buyers who have never seen you

Show eight or ten North American asset managers or engineers the first two screens for five seconds. Ask where the company is based and whether it works on assets like theirs. If they cannot answer the second question, change 1 is worth shipping.

Mine your first North American sales calls

Your sales calls are the cheapest research you have. Note the first question a North American buyer asks that an Australian buyer would not. If it is "do you have customers near me," the case for changes 1 to 3 is made.

Split the homepage by region for a short test

Show a North American visitor the version with the added line and the tagged quote, and measure demo clicks per visitor against the current page. This needs enough North American traffic to mean something inside a few weeks, so pair it with a source you control, such as a webinar or a partner mention.

What I couldn't check

The product and the login area sit behind a login, and the demo sits behind a booking form, so none of this reflects the in-product experience. I do not have your analytics, so I cannot say how many visitors come from North America or how they convert. I saw no region switch on the pages I read and no United States version of the site, but a regional version could exist that I did not find, and a site can show different content by visitor location. The pages were checked from one browser on October 14, 2026. I looked only at the first page of the case-study index, and I do not know what each customer has agreed to let you say about where the work happened.
