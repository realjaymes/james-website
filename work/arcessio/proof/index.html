Built for Arcessio

Where engineers ask, and who answers them

James Praise · Growth and Go-to-Market

I built this workflow and ran it on 15 September 2026. The code is public at github.com/realjaymes/ai-answer-visibility-audit. AI answers change from run to run, so treat the numbers as a snapshot, and the workflow as the part that keeps working.

The workflow

What I built

Your posting asks for AI-powered systems that let a small team perform like a much larger one, and for something built. So I built a small audit workflow and pointed it at the question that matters most for the engineer track: when an engineer asks how to quote, make or source a part, does Arcessio show up in the answer?

Step 1Collect questions17 seed searches expanded through Google autocomplete into 116 real queries engineers type.
Step 2Ask an AI with web search12 questions mapped to Arcessio's agents, each answered by an OpenAI model with live search. Every company and source it cites is recorded.
Step 3Crawl the siteEvery page in arcessio.ai's sitemap, fetched once as an AI crawler sees it and once in a real browser.
Step 4Score and recommendBrand mentions, citation share by domain, readable words per page, and content bets tied to the gaps.

It's under 300 lines of standard-library Python plus the Codex command-line tool and headless Chrome, and a full run takes around 15 minutes. The code is public on GitHub. Rerun weekly, it turns "are we showing up in AI answers" into a number you can track.

What it found

1. Arcessio doesn't appear in AI answers, even for its own category

0 of 12answers mentioned Arcessio
9 of 12answers cited Xometry
8 of 12answers cited Protolabs

The sharpest result came from the question closest to Arcessio's own claim: "Is there AI software that reads engineering drawings or CAD models and recommends how to manufacture a part and which supplier to use?" The answer named Xometry's Instant Quoting Engine as "the closest match," then Paperless Parts. The category Arcessio is building is currently being credited to a marketplace.

2. The companies that win are answering one practical question per page

The pages the model cited weren't brand manifestos. They were a cost calculation guide, a 3D printing vs CNC comparison, a CNC design guide, a DFM checklist, a help-center page on manufacturability analysis, and capability pages that explain what to upload. Xometry also has a page on machine learning for manufacturing, and it was cited on both AI category questions. Each of these pages answers one question an engineer already has, in plain language, which makes it easy for an AI model to quote.

3. AI crawlers can barely read arcessio.ai

The site renders its content in JavaScript. A browser shows the full page, but a crawler that doesn't run JavaScript, which describes most AI answer-engine crawlers, sees little more than the page title.

4. The existing content speaks to enterprise and defense buyers, and none of it answers an engineer's question

All 11 articles in the sitemap cover legacy aftermarket inventory, 3D printing adoption and defense readiness, several from the Accio3D era. They suit the enterprise and Department of Defense track. None of them answers the questions engineers actually search: how to get a part quoted, which process to use, what a drawing needs, or which material fits. Even the defense question went to DLA and America Makes rather than Arcessio's own defense articles, which is what you'd expect when a crawler can't read them.

What I'd do

Five content bets for the engineer track

Each bet answers a question from the audit and shows one of Arcessio's agents doing the work, so the page earns its citation and demonstrates the product in the same place.

1. CNC, 3D printing or sheet metal: a decision guide by part geometryAnswers the process question, with a real part run through the Manufacturability and Material & Equipment agents as the worked example.
2. What a shop needs to quote your part: the STEP file and drawing checklistAnswers the quoting questions Xometry and Protolabs currently own, and ends in a drawing upload.
3. The tolerance callouts that quietly double a quoteA design for manufacturing piece built on the tolerance checks the agent already scores.
4. Materials for functional parts, chosen by requirementThe PEEK, PA12-CF and polycarbonate matching on the homepage, turned into a guide an engineer can cite.
5. What AI can decide from a CAD model todayA plain explanation of the category question, with a sample agent output, so the next answer to "is there AI that reads drawings" has a page to cite besides a marketplace.

Week one

First, make the homepage and resources readable without JavaScript, following the For Suppliers page. Second, publish bet number two, because quoting questions carry the clearest buying intent. Third, run this audit weekly with a wider question set and track Arcessio's share of AI citations as the leading metric for the engineer track, ahead of traffic and alongside signups.

The same workflow scales to the enterprise track by swapping the question set for procurement and defense sourcing questions.
