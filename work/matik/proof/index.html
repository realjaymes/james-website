A plan for Matik

From matik.ai launch to meetings

James Praise · Growth, go-to-market and AI systems

I built this from public sources only, checked on 10 November 2026. I haven't seen your HubSpot data, pipeline or budget, so every number below that depends on them is a question for the first month.

You shipped the biggest release in Matik's history on 21 October. This plan covers the first 90 days of turning it into qualified meetings for your sales development representatives (SDRs) and sales team. It uses three programs, one set of handoff rules in HubSpot and a short list of tests.

What launched, and who it is for

The matik.ai release added agents that build, tailor and check templates, context from call recordings and customer relationship management (CRM) notes, and Feedback Loops, which turn the edits reps make into template recommendations. Your homepage sums up the idea: "Not AI vs. deterministic. AI + deterministic."

Nikola's post on customer segments gives demand generation its two audiences. Long-tail accounts are "almost entirely deterministic". Dedicated enterprise accounts move toward "a 70/30 split", with the last 30% coming from the rep. Okta's digital customer success program and Asana's dedicated team show each end.

Program 1: a live Feedback Loops session

Feedback Loops is the part of the release a rival can't copy quickly, and it is easiest to understand when someone shows it. I'd run one 45-minute live session with a customer admin walking through a real template change, followed by questions.

The invite list starts with your summit and webinar registrants already in HubSpot, filtered to accounts that fit. Paid LinkedIn promotion goes to heads of customer success (CS) and CS operations at the target accounts in program 2. Every registrant from a target account gets an SDR call within one business day.

The recording becomes the first email in a four-step nurture journey. The other three steps are the results page, a template from your library, and a short note from the account's SDR.

Appendix: measures

Main measure. Meetings held and pipeline created from marketing-sourced leads, reported by program and by tier in HubSpot each week.

Early signals. Live session registrations from target accounts, show rate, SDR connect rate within a day, and the share of leads the SDRs accept.

Budget rule. Paid spend on a tier pauses if its cost per meeting held is well above the other tier's after four weeks, and the money moves to the tier that is working.

Appendix: what I'd want to learn in the first 30 days

How many meetings the SDRs book each month and from which sources. How today's pipeline splits between the two tiers. How large and recent the summit and webinar lists are. Which rival comes up most in sales calls. What paid budget exists, and what has been tried. Which customers would join a live session.
