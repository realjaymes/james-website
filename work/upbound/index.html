Hello Upbound

Modelplane needs a name for its category before someone else picks one

Kubernetes won the orchestration layer. Terraform won infrastructure-as-code. Nothing has won fleet-level AI inference management yet, and that gap is the actual job behind this posting. Here's how I'd think about it, and what I bring that's real.

James Praise · Marketing and GTM Operator

8years of cumulative GTM and PMM work
30+open-source AI skills I've built and shipped
$10M+revenue pipeline generated

Why This, Why Now

Modelplane launched in the open in June 2026. That's the easy part. The hard part is the gap you're hiring into: the moment between an open-source drop and a commercial launch, where someone has to define what the paid product actually is before the product team has locked specs, without spending the trust the Crossplane community already gave you.

You've run this exact playbook once, with Crossplane to Upbound Platform v3. That's the strongest signal in the whole posting. It tells me the person you hire isn't inventing a process, they're executing a proven one a second time, faster, with three buyer segments (neoclouds, regulated enterprises, AI-native teams switching off hosted APIs) who each need a different argument for the same product.

The Pitch: My First 30 Days

I wouldn't start by writing positioning. I'd start by listening. Shadow five or more enterprise sales calls to hear how engineers describe the inference problem in their own words, not the words on the website. Interview ten or more Crossplane community members already running inference workloads, so I know who's already feeling fleet-level pain and who hasn't hit it yet. Read everything already written on the Crossplane to Platform v3 transition, because you've already solved half this problem once and I'd rather learn from that than repeat the discovery.

Only after that would I draft the first version of the commercial Modelplane positioning, as a working document shared with product and engineering, not a public launch. Naming the category is a claim you have to be able to defend in an analyst briefing, so it has to survive contact with your own team first.

The Proof

I write for and sell to technical buyers without being the technical person in the room. At Cecure Intelligence, I ran a GTM engine that reached 1,646 MQLs across 5 countries for a cybersecurity product sold to security and compliance teams. I have built 31 open-source Marketing Agent Skills which I actively maintain, real production infrastructure, not a demo. I also built a Prospect & Outbound Engine; a scraping and enrichment pipeline I run myself at real scale, the same instinct to build infrastructure rather than just market it.

Where I'd Need to Catch Up

I don't have hands-on Kubernetes experience, and I won't pretend otherwise. What I have is a pattern of marketing to infrastructure and developer buyers without being the infrastructure person myself, which is a different, learnable skill from being one. I also haven't worked inside a formal open-source-to-commercial org before. I understand the mechanics structurally, the community trust asset, the neutrality constraint, the member who becomes an enterprise buyer, but I'd say that plainly in an interview rather than overstate it. I'd rather name the gap and show you my plan to close it fast than get caught faking depth I don't have.

Let's talk about Modelplane.

If this is the right problem for you, I'd like to talk about it directly. My resume and a note on the specific gaps above are on their way through Greenhouse.
