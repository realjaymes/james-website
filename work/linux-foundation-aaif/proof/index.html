A plan for the Agentic AI Foundation

Three Projects to Ten: A 30-Day Social Calendar for the Agentic AI Foundation

James Praise · Product Marketing Operator

Built only from public pages: aaif.io/projects, the four new projects release, the 300 members release, the MCP 2026-07-28 changelog, the MCP Dev Summit Nairobi schedule and AAIF's own community-events repository. The samples follow the design system in that repository. The calendar runs from Monday 2 November to Tuesday 1 December 2026. It is a proposal for the AAIF team to correct, and every sample is labelled as not published by AAIF.

The plan

What AI answers say about AAIF's projects

On 30 October 2026 I asked three AI answer engines eight questions a developer might ask about AAIF's projects: ChatGPT (through Codex), Perplexity and Google's AI Overview. Then I checked whether each answer named the Agentic AI Foundation. I also checked whether aaif.io appeared in Google's top ten results for each question.

11 of 11answers to “who maintains it” questions credit AAIF
4 of 9answers to “what should I use” questions mention AAIF at all
0 of 8Google top-ten result sets include aaif.io

Asked who maintains the Model Context Protocol, goose or AGENTS.md, every engine names the foundation. Asked which open standard connects agents to tools, ChatGPT answers that MCP is “the open standard created by Anthropic” and links Anthropic's documentation. Asked for a local agent that can use MCP servers, it recommends “Block's Apache-2.0 local agent” and links block.github.io/goose, the address used before goose moved to the aaif-goose organisation. Perplexity names the foundation in none of the three tool-choice answers. Google's top ten for “is there a neutral open source foundation for AI agent standards and protocols” does not include aaif.io.

The top Google result for the first question is a LinkedIn article, and the second is a Facebook group post. Social pages already rank for these questions. That is why every project post in this calendar names the foundation in its first line and links to a foundation page. This was one run on one day with US results, so I would repeat the same eight questions every month and report the trend.

Tue 10 Nov: MCP is now stateless, as a carousel

The carousel is for people who maintain an MCP server. The 2026-07-28 revision is backward-incompatible. Server maintainers need to know what breaks, what replaces it and how long the old features will keep working. Each slide carries the number of the spec enhancement proposal (SEP) behind it, so a reader can check the change for themselves.

Select any slide to open it full size. The carousel opens as a set you can swipe or step through with the arrows.

LinkedIn post with the carousel

The Model Context Protocol, hosted by the Agentic AI Foundation, is now stateless. The 2026-07-28 revision removes the initialize handshake and protocol sessions. If you maintain an MCP server, four things change.

Every request now carries its protocol version and client capabilities in _meta, and a version mismatch returns UnsupportedProtocolVersionError.

The Mcp-Session-Id header is gone. A server that needs state across calls mints its own handle and passes it as an ordinary tool argument.

Servers must now answer server/discover. It advertises the versions, capabilities and identity they support.

Roots, Sampling and Logging are deprecated, and the new policy gives every deprecation at least twelve months before removal.

The full changelog links every proposal behind these changes: https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/changelog

Hritik Raj walks through the change in his keynote at MCP Dev Summit Nairobi on 19 November: https://events.linuxfoundation.org/mcp-dev-summit-nairobi/

The listening read

Three conversations from the last few months are worth joining, and one is worth leaving to the maintainers. For each one I name the thread and draft the reply the AAIF account would post under the publisher's share of it.

Thread 1: “Is stateless MCP just an API now” under the share of InfoQ's August piece

Why join: developers asking this are deciding whether to migrate, and a clear answer from the foundation helps them more than a debate does.

It is closer to an API in one way, because each request now stands on its own. Every server still answers server/discover with its versions, capabilities and identity, so a client can see what it offers before calling it. The changelog: https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/changelog

Thread 2: “Anthropic's Model Context Protocol moves to a stateless model” under the share of TechNewsDaily's 2 September piece

Why join: the headline credits Anthropic ten months after the donation, and the article body already says MCP moved to AAIF. A friendly note under the share fixes the part most people read.

Thanks for covering the change. For readers, MCP has been hosted by the Agentic AI Foundation, part of the Linux Foundation, since Anthropic contributed it in December 2025. Its governance is public here: https://modelcontextprotocol.io/community/governance

Thread 3: AGENTS.md vs CLAUDE.md vs Skills under the share of this comparison

Why join: two of the three things being compared are now open formats hosted by the foundation, and most readers do not know that.

AGENTS.md and Agent Skills are open formats hosted by the Agentic AI Foundation. AGENTS.md gives coding agents instructions for a repository. Agent Skills packages expertise an agent can load for a task. Both work across agents: https://agents.md/ and https://aaif.io/projects/

Leave to the maintainers: MCP security reports for example the April STDIO dispute

Vulnerability reports and disputes about intended behaviour are for the maintainers and the press office to answer, not a social account. When one comes up, I would flag it to them the same hour, hold any scheduled MCP posts that could read as tone-deaf, and share only the guidance the maintainers publish, once they publish it.

What the agents do

AAIF already uses this pattern for chapter events. Its community-events repository gives organisers agent skills that draft LinkedIn posts, event page copy, carousel copy and recaps, with a person approving each one. I would run the main accounts the same way, in four steps.

First, an agent reads the sources each morning: the AAIF blog and news pages, project release notes, member and ambassador posts, and the threads in the listening set. It brings back candidate posts, each with its source. Second, once a candidate is picked, it drafts the post in the AAIF voice for each platform. Third, a checking agent compares every name, number, date and quote in the draft with its source and flags anything it cannot match. It also checks that each project is credited to the foundation. Fourth, I review every draft before it publishes, and anything technical goes to the project's maintainers first.

This is the workflow I already run for my own marketing. My content calendar runs on scheduled agents that read X twice a day and send me candidate posts, then publish the ones I approve on four channels. The Marketing In Action newsletter reaches 5,000+ subscribers in 86 countries, and each issue becomes Substack, X and LinkedIn drafts that I review before they publish. I publish 34 of the 81 agent skills I use as Marketing Agent Skills, and the repository had 287 unique cloners in the last 14 days.

What I would report

A one-page report every two weeks, with a recommendation at the end. It would show reach and engagement rate by post type and platform, and clicks to project docs, the AAIF Briefing and event registration through tagged links. Registrations from social would be counted separately for each event. The listening set would show replies posted, the share of them that got a response, and conversations passed to maintainers.

Two lines are specific to AAIF. The first is a balance count of posts per project and mentions per member each month. It shows when any project or company is getting more than its share of coverage. The second is the eight-question check from the top of this page, rerun every month, to see whether the answers start crediting the foundation when people ask what to use.

Why I structured it this way

Credit the foundation every time

The foundation's projects are well known, and the foundation behind them is less so. When people ask who governs MCP, every engine knows the answer. When they ask what to use, most answers still name the company that started the project. A social account can't fix search on its own, but it controls how its posts credit each project, and the results above show social pages already rank when people search these questions. So the calendar has a rule: the foundation goes in the first line, the contributing company is the origin, and the link goes to a foundation page. The calendar leans on Nairobi and the new projects because those are the next real moments. It ends on the anniversary because that is the clearest way to tell the story of three projects becoming ten.

Day-one questions

I would start by learning the approval path for each kind of post, and which posts need a maintainer's sign-off. I would ask which accounts each project runs for itself, so the main accounts amplify them instead of competing with them. I would also ask how the team balances mentions of Platinum members against everyone else, and what the current report looks like, so my first one matches it.
