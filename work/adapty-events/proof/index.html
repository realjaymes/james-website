How it runs

The go or no-go test

Every event gets one line in HubSpot before money is committed. Three questions decide it.

First, how many named target accounts do we expect in the room, and who at Sales owns each one. If nobody can name them, the event does not go ahead.

Second, what must it return to pay back. The test divides the fully loaded cost, including travel and staff days, by your gross margin on a first-year contract. That gives the number of contracts needed. Your first-year contract value and win rate are the inputs I would need from you [proof needed].

Third, what happened last time. A format earns a repeat when it met its target twice. It gets cut when it misses twice, unless Sales can name a deal the numbers missed.

The motion with Sales

Four weeks before the event, Sales and marketing agree the account list. Two weeks before, each account owner has an invitation out and a meeting request in. After the event, the account owner contacts each attendee within two working days, and that deadline is reported per owner. The weekly review looks at one HubSpot view: event, cost, target accounts present, meetings held, pipeline and closed-won.

What I would do first with London

Your hiring post names a flagship conference in London. I would use AIM as the reference point, because your post describes it as a closed conference and it has already shown you can fill a serious room. I would set three numbers before choosing a venue: the 50 target accounts who must attend, the sponsor revenue that covers direct cost, and the pipeline it must create. The App Leaders Meetups in Berlin and Istanbul were reported at 120 or more people in Berlin and 130 or more in Istanbul, so I would not plan for a large audience until those three numbers exist.

What I would need from you

I would ask for cost per event for the last 12 months, event influence on deals in HubSpot, first-year contract value by segment and sales cycle length. With those four, the table above gets real numbers within the first month.
