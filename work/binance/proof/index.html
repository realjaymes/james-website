A teardown for Binance Africa

Where Binance Loses the Answer in Africa

James Praise · Marketing and GTM Operator, Lagos

On 17 September 2026 I ran 12 Google searches across Nigeria, Kenya, South Africa, Ghana, Cameroon and Côte d'Ivoire, in English, Swahili, French and Hausa, plus one check on Perplexity. For each one I recorded who ranks, whether Google shows an AI answer, and which sources that answer relies on. This uses no internal Binance data and takes no view on Binance's legal position in any country.

The findings

The short version

Where Binance has published a current, local answer, AI answers use it. Google answers "is Binance legal in South Africa" from Binance's own Binance Square post. "Best crypto exchange in Kenya" names Binance first because of its M-Pesa P2P market. In Ghana, a Binance P2P page is the top result for buying USDT with MTN Mobile Money.

Nigeria looks different. It's the region's largest crypto market, with $92.1 billion received from July 2024 to June 2025, and Binance didn't own the answer to any of the five Nigerian searches I ran, including whether Binance still works there.

Nigeria

Is Binance still working in Nigeria

Google's AI answer says Binance only works partially in Nigeria and draws mainly on Breet's June 2026 article. Breet is a crypto-to-naira cash-out app, and that article is also the first organic result. The only Binance page cited is the March 2024 announcement that ended naira services. Perplexity's answer to the same question uses 10 sources, and none of them is binance.com.

The most important brand question in Binance's biggest African market is answered by a company selling the naira cash-out Binance no longer offers there.

Best crypto exchange in Nigeria

The AI answer leads with Quidax on its SEC provisional licence, then lists Bybit, Yellow Card, Luno and CoinCola. Binance isn't named, and no Binance page appears in the top eight organic results.

How to buy USDT in Nigeria

Quidax ranks first. Binance's own page is the last web result on page one, below an April 2024 roundup that still recommends Paxful.

Paxful alternative Nigeria

The AI answer names Binance P2P as an alternative that supports naira transactions, citing a third-party blog. Binance delisted naira P2P pairs in February 2024. A wrong answer in Binance's favour is still a problem. Users arrive expecting something that isn't there, and it's the kind of claim that ends up in a screenshot.

Hausa

Searching "how to buy bitcoin in Nigeria" in Hausa returns YouTube tutorials, a 2021 BBC Hausa article and Facebook posts, some inviting people into WhatsApp and Telegram groups. Google's Hausa AI summary recommends Bybit P2P and Remitano. I ran two site searches on binance.com, one for "hausa" and one for the Hausa phrase "yadda ake." Both return only community posts on Binance Square, including promotions for a third-party token presale. I found no Binance-authored Hausa guide.

Outside Nigeria

Kenya

Binance wins the English "best exchange" answer. In Swahili, no Swahili-language page from anyone ranks organically. Binance's English post "Buy Bitcoin in Kenya via M-Pesa" sits fourth, and the top of the page is Swahili YouTube videos, several from 2021 and one about Paxful.

South Africa

For "buy bitcoin South Africa," Luno ranks above the AI answer and the answer mentions Luno first. For "is Binance legal in South Africa," Binance's own Binance Square post supplies the answer. That second result shows that a current, plainly written Binance page gets used.

Ghana

A Binance P2P page ranks first for buying USDT with MTN Mobile Money. The AI answer cites Binance P2P but names Remitano, HIROPAY and Mybitstore in the answer itself.

Francophone West and Central Africa

In Cameroon, local mobile-money players HIROPAY and Pursa rank above Binance's P2P page. In Côte d'Ivoire, the French AI answer leads with BarkaChange and HIROPAY for Orange Money, MTN MoMo, Moov and Wave, and lists Binance under international platforms.

What the pattern says

AI answers use whatever current local page existsThe South African post shows Binance can win this. The losses are in the markets and languages where that page doesn't exist.
People search in payment-rail termsThey ask about M-Pesa, MTN MoMo, Orange Money and Wave. Local players write in those terms and win the francophone answers.
Language gaps get filled by someone elseWhere there's no official Swahili or Hausa content, YouTube and Facebook groups answer. In crypto that's a user-safety problem as well as a growth problem.
Old third-party claims travel into AI answersA 2024 list still recommending Paxful ranks in Nigeria, and a third-party blog's naira claim about Binance P2P made it into Google's answer.

The plan

How I'd run the first 90 days

Days 1 to 30: measure and correct. I'd build a fixed set of 40 to 60 searches per market and language, then capture organic rank, AI answer presence and citations, and Perplexity and ChatGPT citations every week, next to Search Console data split by country and language. I'd list every third-party page carrying a wrong or stale Binance claim and ask those publishers to correct it, which is link building and answer-engine work in the same email. Anything touching Nigeria's status would go through legal, compliance and the global team before it publishes.

Days 31 to 60: publish the answers AI is looking for. I'd write country and payment-rail pages the way people search ("buy USDT with MTN MoMo," "M-Pesa P2P"), in Swahili and French first, and in Hausa once compliance signs off for Nigeria. Affiliate and comparison publishers like Newhedge, Webopedia, Techpoint Africa and NairaCompare would get accurate, current listings to work from.

Days 61 to 90: connect search to paid and to users. The searches and questions that convert would feed local paid keywords and creative briefs. I'd measure past rank: share of AI citations by market, branded search trend, and new users and first trades by country in AppsFlyer. Each monthly report would explain the reason behind the move, and competitor and regulatory signals would go back to the global team.

The Paxful window

Paxful shut down in November 2025, and its users are still choosing where to go. Google already lists Binance P2P as an alternative in Nigeria, where naira P2P isn't available. In Ghana, Kenya and Cameroon, where P2P is live, Binance's P2P pages should answer the "Paxful alternative" question directly, in local language and payment-rail terms.

Why I built it this way

Method and limits

I built this from outside, the way a user meets Binance: typing a question into Google in their own market and language. The searches ran from Lagos using Google's country and language settings for each market, and AI answers change between runs and between people, so this is a snapshot of a pattern rather than a rank report. A Binance page's snippet even changed between two runs a few minutes apart, which is why the plan above measures weekly.

I run the same kind of measurement on my own sites, pulling Search Console and URL Inspection data through Google's APIs on a schedule. That's how I found a site where more than half its pages were unknown to Google seven weeks after launch.
