A brief for Kayzen

The Apps DSP: A Repositioning and Launch Brief

James Praise · Marketing and GTM Operator

This brief uses only what is public: your job posting and your website. I cannot see your win rates, customer mix or pipeline, so every claim that only your own data could support is marked [proof needed]. A demand-side platform (DSP) is the software advertisers use to buy ad space automatically.

The brief

The gap

Your job posting describes Kayzen as "the Apps DSP: an AI-native platform built on its own bidding engine, for buying app inventory across mobile and connected TV screens." That sentence names a category and gives three reasons to pick it.

Your homepage tells a different story. The headline reads "A mobile-first DSP built for" and then a word that rotates. On 14 November I saw apps, eCommerce and media buyers. The page text I read does not mention connected TV, and your AI products sit behind a "kAi" link in the navigation.

A buyer comparing platforms reads the homepage before any sales call. If it says "mobile-first DSP," you are filed with every other mobile DSP, and your stronger claims never get weighed: your own bidding engine, both screens, and AI that works with the tools a team already uses.

Positioning statement

For app developers, agencies, media buyers and ad platforms that need to buy app inventory and see where the money went, Kayzen is the Apps DSP. It runs on its own bidding engine across mobile and connected TV, shows every bid and impression, and connects campaign data to the AI assistants your team already uses.

Three claims carry it. The category name, Apps DSP, is the missing piece. The proof is transparency: your homepage shows bids, wins, impressions and eCPMs, and your pricing page says ad platforms "can take between 30-70% of the budget in ad tech fees" while Kayzen charges a monthly software fee. The method is AI with people in control, as your kAI Suite page says.

The line that separates Kayzen from other mobile DSPs needs your win and loss data: [proof needed].

Homepage hero options

A. The Apps DSP.

SubheadBuy app inventory across mobile and connected TV, with every bid in view. Buying reasonOne platform for both screens, named as a category a buyer can search for and shortlist.

B. See every bid your app campaigns win.

SubheadThe Apps DSP with bid-level and impression-level transparency, self-serve or managed. Buying reasonTrust. It speaks to the buyer who has been burned by a dashboard that could not explain a result.

C. Run your app campaigns from the assistant you already use.

SubheadThe Apps DSP connects your campaign data to the AI assistants your team works in. Buying reasonSpeed. It is the newest claim and the one competitors cannot copy quickly.

I recommend A. The category name is the thing the page lacks today, and B's transparency proof moves into A's subhead. C waits for product confirmation: the kAI Suite page describes kAI Explorer, kAI Companion and kAI Core, and I did not see the connection to outside AI assistants described there.

90-day launch

The first month is listening, because the message has to survive contact with buyers before it reaches the homepage. The second month ships it everywhere a buyer or a seller sees it. The third month spreads it and checks whether it worked.

Measurement

I would judge the launch on four numbers. The first is demo requests per 1,000 homepage visitors, split by buyer group. The second is the share of first calls where the prospect uses the words "Apps DSP." The third is win rate on deals opened after launch against deals opened before it. The fourth is how often sales actually uses the new deck. Baselines for all four are [proof needed].

What I need from you

Everything marked [proof needed] depends on internal data: retention and return on ad spend from named customer campaigns, agency retention, win rate and eCPM against alternatives, a named platform partner, and the baselines for the four measures. I would collect these in the first 30 days and decide which claims earn a place on the homepage.

I would also confirm two product facts before using them in public: that connected TV buying is live in the platform, and that kAI connects to outside AI assistants. Both come from your posting. I did not find either described on the pages I read.

What I read

I read your job posting, your homepage with its Solutions, Product and Resources menus open, and your kAI Suite page and your pricing page, all on 14 November 2026. The Product menu lists Kayzen DSP, kAI Suite, Video+ and Pricing. I did not read the product pages behind the menus, so nothing here says what they do or do not contain.

My own work behind this approach: at Reform, I rewrote the positioning for technical and business buyers. Trial activation rose 34% and trial-to-paid conversion rose 21%.
