ResumeWise search funnel: a first read

Your ResumeWise ads sell "Free." Your profit comes from the $29.95 renewal.

James Praise · Paid acquisition and growth

The read

Intro

Your Head of PPC posting asks for someone to optimize funnel performance across your US subscription products. I looked at the one product I could tie to FutureMedia from public sources, ResumeWise, whose site footer names Futuremedia EOOD in Sofia. I have not seen your accounts or your numbers, so treat everything below as a test we would run together.

What the ads say

Google's Ads Transparency Center lists nine US text ads for ResumeWise under your verified advertiser, Futuremedia EOOD. Eight of the nine headlines say "Free," for example "Resume Builder (Free) - Free Resume Builder" and "Word & PDF Download (Free)." The sitelinks read "Start Your Resume (free)."

What the product costs

The ResumeWise pricing page has no free download. Seven days of full access cost $1.95, and seven days of limited access cost $1.45. Both renew at $29.95 a month after seven days. The homepage itself never uses the word "free," so the promise sits in the ads and the price sits on your site.

What reviewers say

ResumeWise holds a strong 4.6 rating from 2,130 reviews on Trustpilot. Trustpilot's own summary of those reviews still says some reviewers were unhappy about unexpected charges at the end of the process, and that many mention paying to download the finished resume. Its price topic says some reviewers report unexpected subscription charges and unclear pricing terms.

Why it matters for bidding

A $1.95 trial barely pays for the click that bought it. You make money when a trial renews at $29.95 and is not refunded. If your Google and Bing accounts bid toward trial starts, they will keep buying the clicks that start trials, and "Free" headlines are very good at that. Some of those people expected a free tool, so they cancel or ask for a refund, and the ad platform never sees it.

You may already send renewals back to Google. If so, this becomes a quick check. If not, the bidding is learning from the cheapest event in your funnel instead of the one that pays.

The policy question

Google's Misrepresentation policy lists "dishonest pricing practices" and says ads must clearly disclose the payment model and the full cost a user will bear. I have not seen your checkout, so I can't say whether ResumeWise meets that bar. "Free" headlines for a paid subscription are what a policy review looks at, and a suspension would stop the whole advertiser account. I would review this with your copywriting team in the first week.

The plan

Cost per paying subscriber

This is the number I would put at the center of every decision. Enter your own figures. The starting values are made up for illustration and are not ResumeWise's numbers.

Ad cost per paying subscriber: $0

First-month revenue per trial, $1.95 plus kept renewals: $0

Cost per paying subscriber = cost per trial ÷ (renewal rate × (1 − refund rate)). Two campaigns with the same cost per trial can be far apart here, which is why I would bid on it.

What I'd do first

Teach the bidding what pays. I would send the first renewal, net of refunds, back to Google Ads and Microsoft Advertising as an offline conversion, an event recorded after the click and uploaded with its dollar value. Trial starts stay as a secondary signal for volume. Value-based bidding, where the platform bids more for clicks likely to bring more revenue, then learns which searches, headlines and audiences bring subscribers who keep paying.

Test an honest price headline. One arm keeps "Free." The other says "7 days for $1.95" or similar. I expect fewer trials from the honest arm, so the winner is the arm with the lower cost per renewed subscriber and fewer refunds. Either result lowers your policy risk or proves the current copy pays for it.

Use one scorecard on every channel. Bing can take the same renewal import. Taboola and Quora reach people before they search, so they need their own landing pages and a renewal target from day one. Traffic from these native ad networks can look cheap on trials and still renew badly.

A 90-day plan

Days 1 to 30: learn the accounts. Map every conversion action to what it really counts. Pull trial, renewal and refund rates by campaign, keyword theme and headline. Review the checkout and the "Free" copy against Google's policy with your copywriters. Set up the renewal import on the largest Google and Bing campaigns.

Days 31 to 60: move the bidding. Run renewal-value bidding as a campaign experiment on part of the budget before rolling it out. Launch the honest price headline test, and match landing pages to each headline with your design team.

Days 61 to 90: scale with the same rule. Take the scorecard to Taboola, Quora and your other products. Build one forecast model by product and channel, based on cost per renewed subscriber and expected months retained, so you can see how much more spend each product can take.

How I'd measure it. Cost per renewed subscriber by product and channel comes first, then months to pay back acquisition cost. Refund rate, ad disapprovals and trial volume act as guardrails.

Where I've done this

Judging ads on backend sales. On my own business's Meta account, the pixel (the tracking code on the site) credited sales to the wrong campaigns. I switched to the backend sales export, which showed the leak was at checkout. That account runs at 4.84x return on ad spend, and I published the method in a Paid Acquisition Playbook covering 8 ad platforms.

Running a consumer account for years. At JegzFits Leatherworks, a consumer brand, I have run Meta and TikTok acquisition since 2021. Between May and November 2026 its monthly revenue more than doubled while return on ad spend rose to about 8x.

Trial-to-paid work. At Reform I launched a Pro Plan free trial across web, email and in-app, and trial-to-paid conversion rose 21%.

Search and social budgets. At Cecure Intelligence, a cloud and training company in five African countries, I ran Google and Meta on $1.5K to $15K a month at 2 to 3x return on ad spend.
