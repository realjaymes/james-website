A plan for CEA

When the News Arrives First: Answering the Headline Questions on effectivealtruism.org

James Praise · Marketing, communications and brand

This plan uses only public sources: your job posting, your strategy and progress posts on the EA Forum, recent coverage, and effectivealtruism.org, all read on 24 November 2026. It proposes which questions to answer, not the answers. Those are yours to decide. Anything only your data can settle is marked [proof needed].

The plan

The moment

This autumn, The Economist put effective altruism (EA) on its cover. Fortune covered US political attacks that named EA over AI policy. A Netflix series on FTX is coming.

Before this wave, Rethink Priorities found about 12% of US adults said they had heard of EA, and only 1 to 2% understood it when tested. So for most people, a headline is the first thing they learn.

"By not actively advocating for or defending ourselves, we've let critics define us." CEA's 2025–26 strategy

Zachary told AFP that CEA can "do a better job explaining." This plan is about where that explaining happens first.

What a newcomer finds

I went to effectivealtruism.org as a reader coming from one of those stories would.

The FAQ answers other questions.

The FAQ, "Frequent questions & honest objections", lists about 50 questions. They are careful, and mostly about philosophy. None covers FTX, AI companies or politics.

Searching for "FTX" returns "No results".

A search for "Anthropic" only matches the word "philanthropic". Of the 1,486 pages in the site's sitemap, none covers these topics, and /press and /news return "page not found".

The press kit is on another site.

Journalists get a press kit on centreforeffectivealtruism.org. The readers they send arrive at effectivealtruism.org, which has nothing for them.

The site also does a lot right. Its progress report shows newsletter sign-ups from visitors rose from 0.6% to 2.6%. A survey on the site asks "How did you hear about effective altruism?", with "A news article" as one answer. So CEA can already measure the problem.

An answers section for the questions the news raises

I would add a short section with one page per question a news reader arrives with. The list is in the appendix. CEA decides each answer. My job would be the standard every page meets.

StandardWhat it means
ShortThe answer in the first two sentences, detail below.
SourcedEvery claim links to its evidence, including CEA's own past statements.
Honest about mistakesWhere EA got something wrong, the page says so and says what changed.
DatedEach page shows when it was last checked, and is reviewed when the news moves.
A next stepEach page ends with one way to learn more: the newsletter, a course or a talk.

Each page also works as the briefing for spokespeople and comms teams at EA organizations, so a journalist gets the same true answer from whoever picks up. And it gives journalists a page to link to.

Measuring who the news brings in

I would run one weekly report that follows a news wave from coverage to action.

StepWhat to count
CoverageStories that week, their reach and tone.
InterestSearches for "effective altruism" and visits from news sites.
ArrivalThe share of survey answers that say "A news article".
UnderstandingVisits to the answers pages, and how far people read.
ActionNewsletter and course sign-ups from those visitors, set against visitors sent by a friend or group.

The last row is the one that matters. It shows whether news attention turns into people who stay, or only into traffic. Today's sign-up rate split by source is [proof needed].

A part for each of the three leaders

The role hires leaders of Media, Brand and Content, and Marketing. Each would own one part of this plan, so it runs without the Director in every decision.

Media

Owns the facts on each answers page, the bench of agreed spokespeople, and the response routine for big moments such as the Netflix series.

Brand and Content

Owns the voice of the answers, and the stories that show EA's work: malaria nets, cage-free hens, lead paint rules.

Marketing

Owns the path from an answer to a first action, and the weekly report.

Appendix

The question list

These are the questions I would start with, taken from this year's coverage. The list should change with the survey and search data.

1. What happened with FTX, and what changed in EA afterwards?

2. Is EA mostly about AI now?

3. What is EA's connection to AI companies?

4. Is EA political?

5. Who funds EA, and who decides where the money goes?

6. Is EA a closed group, and what does getting involved actually involve?

What I need from you

What CEA has already decided about speaking on FTX and AI companies, and who approves. The survey results so far. Newsletter and course sign-up rates by how visitors heard about EA. Which EA organizations already coordinate on comms. And how Tess sees her role once this hire starts.
