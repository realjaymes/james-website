The brief I gave myself

One report, run through the job once

The Content Marketing Manager posting asks for a research piece every two weeks, repurposed on LinkedIn through the product owner's and the team's accounts, with webinars run as campaigns. Adapty's newest report is State of Paywalls 2026. It covers 78,000+ paywalls from 30,000+ apps, with data from October 2025 to September 2026. I ran that report through the cycle one time to show what the two weeks would look like.

You get four LinkedIn posts, written for Maxim Borisik, Sergey Muratov and the company page. You get a Luma page for a 30-minute Builder session on the findings, and the follow-up email that goes out after the session.

The four LinkedIn posts

Post 1: the company page, week 1 Monday

Which plan sells best depends on the category you are in.

In State of Paywalls 2026, weekly plans bring 62% of subscription revenue across the apps in the report. Annual plans bring 21% and monthly plans bring 12%.

The average hides the split. Utilities apps earn 90% of their revenue from weekly plans. Health & Fitness apps earn 62% of theirs from annual plans.

Before you copy a competitor's pricing, check which category's pricing you are copying.

The report covers 78,000+ paywalls from 30,000+ apps, with data from October 2025 to September 2026. Link in the comments.

Numbers from the FAQ on State of Paywalls 2026.

Post 2: Maxim's account, week 1 Tuesday

Most teams ask whether the paywall belongs in onboarding or later. The State of Paywalls data says it can sit in both.

Onboarding paywalls convert 0.85% of views to paid. Paywalls shown later convert 0.23%. And 93% of first purchases happen on install day.

Later paywalls still matter. They bring 38% of subscription revenue.

I read those numbers as two jobs. The onboarding paywall catches people who have already decided. The later paywall catches people who needed to use the app first.

In the Flow & Paywall Builder you can put both in one flow and test them against each other without an app store release. Report link in the comments.

Numbers from the FAQ on State of Paywalls 2026. The Builder description follows Adapty's own product positioning, which I have not tested.

Post 3: Sergey's account, week 1 Thursday

A hard paywall starts 48% more trials than a soft one. It also gets 33% fewer direct purchases.

That trade looks like a loss until you add the two together. On combined trials and purchases, a hard paywall wins in 57% of apps. Only 11% of apps use one.

It does not win in the rest, so your own app decides. That is why the report comes with a Claude skill. You can check your paywall against the data instead of reading a chart and guessing.

Maxim is running a 30-minute Builder session on these findings next Thursday. Registration is in the comments.

Numbers from the FAQ on State of Paywalls 2026, which also lists the Claude skill as a getting-started step.

Post 4: Maxim's account, week 2 Monday

Thursday I'm running a 30-minute session in the Flow & Paywall Builder, built on the State of Paywalls 2026 data.

We will put an onboarding paywall and a later paywall in one flow, set up the test, and decide what to test first. The data points to offers before visuals. Localization tests raised lifetime value (LTV) in 62% of cases, trial tests in 60% and plan duration tests in 59%. Visual and copy tests did it in 35%.

Bring a paywall you want a second opinion on. Registration link in the comments.

After it ships

The weekly lead report

The posting asks for weekly reporting on which content brings leads. For this campaign I would track four things per post: clicks on its tagged link, report form submissions, Luma registrations and Builder sign-ups that trace back to it. The report would show which of the four posts moved people to the next step and which only collected reactions. I would use that to choose the angle for the next two-week cycle.

Why it is built this way

The job is a steady rhythm around one product, so I built one full cycle instead of a plan about cycles. State of Paywalls is Adapty's newest report, and the testing chapter is the strongest argument for the Builder, so the session leans on it. I split the four posts across three accounts because the posting says the role writes for the product owner and the team. The next cycle would be a research piece built on data only Adapty holds, such as what teams change in their first onboarding flow.
