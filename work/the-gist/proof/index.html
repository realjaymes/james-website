A proposal module for The GIST

The "Non-Endemic Brand" Module: A Reusable RFP Opener

James Praise · Integrated Marketing Manager, applicant

Built from The GIST's own public surfaces: Jacie deHoop's Marketing Brew interview (October 2024) and the audience data on the live Partner with Us page, checked on 2026-09-17. Anything that needs data I don't have access to as an outside applicant is marked [proof needed] rather than guessed.

The module

The thesis

Every RFP for a brand that has never sponsored sports before starts from the same objection: "we're not a sports brand, why would we sponsor sports content." Jacie deHoop already has the answer, on the record:

"We certainly bring on a lot of sports-endemic partners, but it's been so fun to work with that next layer of partners too, that maybe historically haven't really put their stake in a sports tentpole or in a sponsorship." — Jacie deHoop, Co-Founder & Head of Partnerships and Revenue, Marketing Brew, October 2024

That line is the argument. It just hasn't been turned into a section a seller can drop into a new RFP on a deadline. This module does that: it takes the thesis, backs it with the audience data The GIST already publishes, and gives it a repeatable shape.

The audience case

The case for a first-time sponsor isn't "we cover sports." It's who is reading, and what they do because of what they read. Three numbers from The GIST's own Partner with Us page carry the argument:

83%of GISTers say they choose brands that support women's sports and media
94%of women in C-suite roles played sports growing up, a buying-power signal, not just a reach number
91%are interested in brands The GIST recommends to them directly

Paired with reach (1M newsletter subscribers at a 45% open rate, 582K social followers, 45M+ GISTers reached monthly across newsletter, social and podcast, all per the same live page), the pitch to a first-time category isn't "you'll get seen." It's "this specific audience already tells you, in writing, that showing up here changes what they buy."

Precedent

This isn't a theory. The GIST has already closed non-endemic categories against this exact thesis: Bumble, Ally, Heineken Silver, and SpringHill Suites by Marriott, named in the same Marketing Brew interview as sponsors that hadn't historically bought into sports. The current Partner with Us page carries a live testimonial from SpringHill Suites on the results. That's the proof a new RFP can point to: not "we think this works," but "here's who already said yes, and what they got."

A worked opener

Here's the module in the shape a seller would actually paste into a deck, with the bracketed fields being the only thing that changes per RFP:

A seller drops in the brand name, picks the sharpest stat for that brand's specific objection, and the opening page of the proposal is done in minutes instead of rebuilt from a blank slide.

Why I structured it this way

Reusable, not rebuilt

The JD's actual ask is throughput: turning brand interest into a polished, on-brand, deadline-driven proposal fast enough to win the deal, for a Partnerships team that's grown headcount faster than it's grown proposal capacity. A one-off pitch for a hypothetical brand would show I can write a good proposal. A reusable module shows I understand the actual bottleneck, which is that the same argument gets rebuilt from scratch every time a new non-obvious category comes in. I built the fixed and localized columns as a real production template because that's the difference between writing one proposal well and building the thing that makes every proposal faster.

I didn't build a full mock RFP response or audit The GIST's own partner-facing page, both real options I considered. The GIST already runs a live, well-built Partner with Us page with its own media kit request flow, so a page audit would have been telling a team something they already know about their own site. This module does something narrower and more useful: it takes a thesis their own co-founder has already stated publicly and turns it into the specific, reusable asset that thesis doesn't have yet.
