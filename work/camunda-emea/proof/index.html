A brief for Camunda

The EMEA co-sell motion for SAP-shop accounts

James Praise · Marketing and GTM Operator

Prepared for Justin Vaughan-Brown, VP Product Marketing & Strategic Communications. Written from public sources only, so each claim names its source, and the last section lists what I could not confirm from outside.

The motion in one paragraph

Camunda has the hard part of an SAP story already. The integration is built, Camunda's documentation calls it SAP Certified, it runs inside the customer's own SAP Business Technology Platform (BTP) instance, and Panasonic Europe runs it at more than 220,000 workflow instances a year. What I could not find from outside is the motion around it: a sharp account profile, a clear line between Camunda and SAP's own process tools, and a plan for SAP's field and the systems integrators. This brief proposes all three for Europe, the Middle East and Africa (EMEA), with a 90-day plan to launch them.

Who to go after

The account profile has three triggers. An account needs at least one, and the strongest accounts have two.

Trigger 1: ECC on the 2027 clock. Most ECC customers will not finish by the end of 2027, and about half expect to run ECC into 2030, according to the DSAG Investment Report 2026. Those companies will run ECC and S/4HANA side by side for years, and a process layer above both is useful on day one and survives the move. Panasonic's own description, reported by SAPinsider, is that when an ERP system changes, the process itself can remain largely untouched. The DSAG survey covers German-speaking member companies only. The EMEA-wide number is a first-week task.

Trigger 2: a Clean Core mandate. The customer has been told to stop customizing the ERP before it migrates. The approval chains and exception handling that lived in SAP's custom ABAP code have to go somewhere. Camunda's answer is Business Process Model and Notation (BPMN) and Decision Model and Notation (DMN) models that business and IT can both read.

Trigger 3: a process that crosses systems. Panasonic's business-partner process is the example: a request starts in a form, gets validated against SAP, and records an approval path, with S/4HANA staying the source of truth. Quote-to-cash, procure-to-pay, onboarding and claims have the same shape, and any of them that touch a non-SAP system fit, as SAPinsider also notes.

Titles to reach: head of SAP or ERP transformation, enterprise architect, head of process excellence, and the chief information officer. These are hypotheses. The first test is whether Camunda's closed-won deals in EMEA show the same titles.

Where Camunda sits next to SAP's own tools

SAP sells its own answer. SAP Press describes SAP Build Process Automation as a way for business users to automate workflows and tasks without writing code, with decision logic and robotic task automation built in. Joule with SAP Signavio became generally available in February 2026, and SAP says it offers orchestration across SAP applications. SAPinsider put the difference this way in July 2026: SAP's model starts from the suite and extends outward, and Camunda's starts from the process and coordinates across the landscape.

The field needs one rule it can use in a meeting: SAP's tools win inside SAP, and Camunda wins when the process leaves it.

Three objections to prepare for.

"We already have Build Process Automation." Agree with it. Ask which of the customer's processes touch a system SAP does not own, and start with that one. Camunda does not have to replace anything to start.

"We will sort processes out after the S/4HANA move." The process work is what makes the move smaller. Camunda's own claim, in its October 2024 blog, is that moving in stages reduces testing effort and risk. The Panasonic case is the evidence to use, with the caveat that it is Camunda's own case study.

"One more platform to run on BTP." The components deploy into the customer's BTP instance. A CamundaCon 2024 write-up reports that no additional Camunda licensing fee applies for customers already on BTP. The message must not attack SAP. SAP's field is a source of introductions, and the customer's system of record is SAP. The tone is "we make your SAP investment go further."

Partner and field plays

For Camunda's EMEA field

A one-page SAP discovery card: five questions that find a Trigger 1, 2 or 3 account in the first call. A ten-minute demo script: start a Camunda process from a Fiori screen, render a Camunda form in Fiori, read from S/4HANA through the OData connector, and add one agent step that calls an SAP function. One environment, rehearsed, owned by a named solutions engineer. A Panasonic reference kit: a one-page summary, the four numbers, and a call the field can request. The situation table and objection sheet above, built into the sales playbook.

For SAP's field

A short "when to bring Camunda in" note for SAP account executives and BTP specialists, built on the situation table. It leads with their problem: customers who cannot finish on the 2027 clock. Then a joint ninety-minute workshop for a shared customer: map five processes, mark where each touches a non-SAP system, and pick one to build. It ends with a decision. Last, a check on whether an SAP Store listing is available and worth the work. I found none from outside.

For systems integrators

The Panasonic case study names Holisticon and cimt AG as implementation partners, and CamundaCon 2024 coverage mentions a Cognizant accounts-payable solution built on the OData interface. These are the first calls. Give them the demo environment, the discovery card and a clear rule on who owns the customer conversation.

How to tell whether it worked

Leading measures, checked every two weeks: the share of EMEA SAP-tagged opportunities where the discovery card was used, workshops held with named accounts and how many ended with a chosen process, and integrator-sourced opportunities.

Lagging measures, checked at day 90 and day 180: SAP-tagged pipeline created and its win rate against other pipeline, sales cycle length for SAP-tagged deals, and reference calls requested and completed.

I have no baseline for any of these. Week one produces it, and the targets get set from it. [proof needed: Camunda's customer relationship management data]

What I could not confirm from outside

Whether Camunda holds any SAP partner tier above Technology Alliance Partner, and whether SAP's sellers co-sell Camunda today. Whether a Camunda listing exists in the SAP Store; I searched and found none. What share of Camunda's EMEA pipeline already touches SAP. What EMEA means for this role, a product area or a region. SAP's placement in the 2025 Gartner Magic Quadrant for Business Orchestration and Automation Technologies: SAP is among the vendors Gartner assessed, and I did not find its position.

Why this shape

The hand-off

A co-sell motion fails at the hand-off. The SAP seller, the Camunda seller and the integrator each need one thing they can say and one thing they can do in the first meeting. That is why the brief is a rule, a table and a demo script before it is anything else, and why every proof point I could not source is marked instead of rounded up.
