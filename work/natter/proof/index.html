A page rewrite for Natter

Putting Natalie's Launch Proof on the Natalie Page

James Praise · Product Marketing and GTM Operator

I built this from public pages read on 16 November 2026: the Natalie page, the Natalie launch post, the case studies page and the job posting. Every figure on this page comes from Natter's own launch post. I haven't seen your analytics, win-loss notes or customer approvals, so anything that depends on them is marked [proof needed].

The rewrite

The gap

You launched Natalie at the Gartner HR Symposium in October, and the launch post carries the strongest proof Natter has published. Accenture Operations cut the time spent analysing and flagging attrition signals by 83%. Thoughtworks reached an employee Net Promoter Score of +70, a measure of how likely employees are to recommend the experience. Before launch, employees held 12,000 conversations with Natalie.

The Natalie page shows none of it. A Human Resources (HR) leader who met Natalie at your booth and visits the page later reads the claim without the evidence. The posting names customer proof and turning unfamiliar ideas into customer stories as core work, so this is where I would start.

What the page says today

The page has four parts. The hero says "The AI Voice Agent, built for Enterprise." Two paragraphs explain that Natalie gathers new insight instead of answering questions from stored knowledge. A short block covers always-on, confidential conversations. Then the FAQ's six questions cover Natter's platform in general, such as session sizes of up to 20,000 people and uses for sales coaching.

Three things are missing. No customer is named and no result is shown. The FAQ also skips what a buyer asks about an AI voice agent: is it anonymous, will people take part, and how is it different from our survey?

The rewritten page

I kept your page's order and reused your own wording wherever it already worked. Each block below is one section of the page. The second block is a proof band: a strip of three results placed straight under the headline.

Hero

Your survey shows something changed. Natalie finds out why.

Natalie holds spoken, one-to-one conversations with every employee, in 35 languages, at any hour. It follows each person's answer toward the question you set, and Natter turns thousands of conversations into themes, reasons and quotes by the next morning.

Two customer stories

Accenture: finding why people leave. Managers at Accenture Operations already ran regular one-to-ones across more than 80,000 people in India, but reading the answers by hand was slow and uneven. Natalie started each conversation from one question and followed what each person said. It found attrition signals the existing process had missed and cut analysis and flagging time by 83%.

Thoughtworks: testing a new strategy with employees. When the CEO set a new strategic vision, employees discussed it with Natalie in their own words. Leadership saw how well people understood it, whether they could act on it, and where views differed. Employees rated the experience at +70 on the employee Net Promoter Score.

Each story links to a full case study once one is written. Today all four studies on your case studies page cover live sessions.

How Natalie differs

Most AI interviewers work through a fixed list of questions. You give Natalie a goal, such as understanding why people leave. If someone mentions trust in leadership, Natalie asks why. If the answer points to a manager or a recent change, Natalie follows that thread. Every conversation is different, and leaders get reasons and suggested actions, not only scores.

FAQ, rewritten for Natalie

Is it anonymous? Taking part is voluntary. You set the anonymity threshold, personal details are removed automatically, and quotes are shown only where you allow them.

Will employees take part? When Accenture co-designed Natalie, 85% of the employees invited chose to take part, and 91% wanted to talk to Natalie again.

Does it replace our engagement survey? No. Your survey measures how people feel and tracks it over time. Natalie answers the question that follows a score: what is behind it, and what should we do?

How long does it take to roll out? Security and procurement onboarding averages 26 days. After that, the whole organization can use Natalie the same day.

Is our data used to train AI models? Never. Natter tests Natalie across 22 AI safety areas and 61 scenarios, holds ISO 27001 certification, complies with UK and EU data protection law, and offers US-only processing.

The page also needs one name for the category. Your site uses "conversation intelligence", "employee listening" and "employee voice" for the same idea. How I'd know it worked

I would track three numbers before and after the change. The first is demo requests from the Natalie page. The others are the share of visitors who scroll past the proof band, and how often sellers send the page to a prospect. The baselines come from your analytics and your sales team [proof needed]. I would also ask the sellers which of the three results comes up most in calls, and move that one to the front.
