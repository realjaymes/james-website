A brief for Prophecy Social

Positioning Brief: Game, Market, or the Bridge Between Them

James Praise · Product Marketing Operator

Built entirely from Prophecy Social's own public content: prophecy.social, its blog posts on blog.somnia.network, and its public docs.

The brief

Game or market

Prophecy Social's own docs state it plainly: "PST is soulbound. It lives in your account and can't be transferred, traded, or sold." Users earn it through sign-up grants, daily bonuses, and winning predictions. By design, that makes Prophecy Social a free-to-play engagement game, not a real-money financial product. In under a week of launch, it drew 5,000+ users and 2,000+ live markets, every one created and resolved entirely by AI agents with no human moderator.

5,000+users in under a week, a real, fast, proven launch
2legitimate positioning options, neither one stated

The missing comparison

"Prediction markets" as a category has two dominant reference points: Polymarket and Kalshi, both real-money platforms. Anyone crypto-native landing on Prophecy Social will reach for that comparison without being asked to. Checked across the homepage, both blog posts, and the docs: that comparison is never made, not to claim parity, not to draw a deliberate contrast. The product's real differentiators, AI-agent resolution in seconds, zero cost, zero regulatory complexity, never get framed against the category leaders at all.

A product doesn't need to be a prediction market to use the words. It does need to say, somewhere, what it is instead.

The frame that resolves it

Framing optionWhat it does
"The on-ramp to prediction markets"Positions Prophecy Social as the practice layer before real-money markets, using the zero-cost, zero-friction design as the reason, not an apology
Direct Polymarket/Kalshi contrastNames the category leaders explicitly, then states plainly why soulbound points serve a different, complementary purpose

Either framing works. What doesn't work is silence on the comparison a crypto-native visitor is already making in their head. That frame belongs on the homepage hero, the first blog post a new user is likely to read, and any KOL kit built for creator partnerships.

Why I structured it this way

Naming the gap, not faking the domain

I don't have hands-on prediction-markets or protocol marketing experience, and this brief doesn't pretend otherwise. What it does is the part of the role I can credibly own on day one, noticing an unresolved positioning question and building the frame that resolves it. It's the same discipline behind the Trust Wallet trust-surface brief I'm working on this same week, another category I'm new to, closed with evidence instead of assumed fluency.
