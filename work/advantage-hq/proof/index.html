A brief for Advantage HQ

Intelligent Change: The Growth Pod Brief After $1.4M a Month

James Praise · Marketing and GTM Operator

This brief uses only what Intelligent Change and Advantage HQ publish. I can't see the account, the email platform or the retention data, so some of this may already be running. Treat it as the brief I'd bring to a first pod meeting, with questions to check before anything gets built.

The brief

Where the brand is today

Advantage HQ's own case study shows Intelligent Change growing from $650K to $1.4M a month, with ad spend at about $200K a month and a 6.7x return on ad spend maintained along the way. The numbers in that case study are revenue and ad spend. The same homepage describes a customer lifetime value (LTV) pillar, "LTV: Your Cash Multiplier," built on post-purchase funnels, upsells, bundles and email and SMS. This brief applies that pillar to the brand's hero product.

$650K to $1.4M a monthRevenue growth in the Advantage HQ case study, with a 6.7x return on ad spend maintained.
6 monthsHow long The Five Minute Journal lasts, per its own product page ("Undated, lasts 6 months"). The money-back guarantee also runs 6 months.
5 customer countsDifferent figures for how many people the brand has reached, across the homepage, product page and meta description.

The month-six reorder gap

The Five Minute Journal costs $32 and is built to run out. Its product page says it lasts 6 months, and the packaging photo repeats it. A customer who builds the habit finishes the journal around the time the guarantee window closes.

On the product page and cart I checked on 17 September 2026, there was no subscribe option, no "send my next journal" choice and no reorder prompt. The cart's "You may also like" section recommends one-time bundles instead: the Dream Life Bundle at $63.90, the Morning Routine Bundle at $64.80 and a Pen and Clip Set at $30.60. Those raise the first order's value. None of them brings back a customer at month six.

A journal that runs out on a known date is the easiest reorder in direct-to-consumer. The customer already has the habit, and the brand knows when the pages end.

One customer number

Creator briefs and ads borrow proof from the site. Right now the site gives them five different numbers for the same claim:

WhereWhat it says
Homepage hero"Join 3+ million people starting every day happier."
Homepage, Research-Driven section"We've helped over 4+ million people"
Homepage footer"Over 4,000,000+ products have already been sold worldwide and have helped to create positive change in hundreds of thousands of people's lives."
Meta description"trusted by 4M+ people worldwide"
Product page badge"3+ million sold"
Product page guarantee"We've helped over a million people around the world"
Product page packaging photo"2M+ products sold"

Some of this is products sold against people helped, which are different claims. The fix is small: pick one verified number for each claim, use it everywhere on the site, and put it in every creator brief so a whitelisted ad never quotes a figure the landing page contradicts.

How I'd measure it

Start with the reorder curve before building anything. If repeat purchase at months 5 to 7 is already strong, the flow exists and the work is scaling it with paid and creators. If it's weak, the product page option and the lifecycle flow come first.

Judge it on contribution per customer as well as return on ad spend. A reorder costs far less to win than a new customer, so blended return and 12-month customer value are the numbers that show whether it worked.

Check it against the sales record. Platform-reported conversions run high. I'd reconcile reorders against Shopify orders before calling any test a win.

Method and limits

Every figure here was checked on 17 September 2026 against advantagehq.com, the Intelligent Change homepage and The Five Minute Journal product page, including a live add-to-cart to see the cart recommendations. Nothing was ordered.

I picked Intelligent Change because it's a brand Advantage HQ already grew, so the brief can build on real work. What I can't see is the account, the email platform, the retention numbers or what the brand has already tested, so the table above is a starting plan to check with the pod.
