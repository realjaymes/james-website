A message hierarchy for Kredete

One Story per Buyer

James Praise · Growth and Product Marketing, Lagos

On 5 October 2026 I read Kredete's homepage, credit page and business page in a desktop browser, plus the App Store listing and press coverage of the Series A and the Gravv acquisition. This uses no internal data. Anything that needs Kredete's own numbers is marked proof needed.

Today

The short version

Kredete's homepage sells transfers, a Visa card, dollar savings, credit building, stocks, prediction markets and travel accounts in one scroll. The headline, "The autonomous way to ___ money globally," rotates through six verbs in the same spot: move, spend, receive, save, grow and trade.

Since the Gravv acquisition, two very different buyers land on the same domain: a nurse in Houston sending $300 home, and a finance lead paying suppliers in four currencies. They decide on different proof. So I'd split the story in two, with one lead promise each, and let every other product become a reason to stay.

Diaspora sendersSend money home. Build your US credit.
Business buyersPay and get paid across emerging markets, settled locally.

The hierarchy

Branch 1: Diaspora senders

Positioning. For African immigrants in the US who send money home every month and have little or no US credit history, Kredete is the money app that turns those transfers into a US credit file. The other transfer apps compete on rate and speed. Kredete makes money you already send count toward your credit score, and the rate, card and savings make it the app you keep.

Why credit leads. It is the founding story: Adeola Adedewe was turned down for 17 credit cards after moving to the US. It is the one claim in the category that no other transfer app leads with. And the credit page is already the clearest page on the site.

What leads, and what keeps them

  1. Lead: send money home and build US credit from it.
  2. First reason to stay: the rate shown up front. The homepage already promises "the number on screen is exactly what they receive."
  3. Second: rent reporting, a second way to build the same score.
  4. Third: the Visa card and dollar savings.
  5. In the app, off the sender homepage: stocks, ETFs and prediction markets. Someone deciding who to trust with family money may read a featured basketball market as a different kind of product. That's a hypothesis to test, not a verdict.

By market

  • US senders: credit leads, because the credit product builds a US file and starts with an SSN.
  • UK and EU senders: US credit doesn't apply. Lead with the rate and the local account. The homepage already has the copy for it ("GBP account live," "SEPA in. SEPA out.").
  • Receivers in Nigeria, Ghana and Kenya: a separate, later story, kept off the sender homepage.

Send money home. Build your US credit.

Every eligible transfer to family can count toward your US credit score. See exactly what they receive before you send. proof needed: corridors and bureaus

A GBP account, a Visa card and transfers to 60+ markets in one app.

Branch 2: Business buyers

Positioning. For finance leads at businesses that pay and get paid across emerging markets, Kredete for Business is a multi-currency operating account running on its own settlement rails. A bank wire or a patchwork of payout providers can't settle locally in 60+ countries from one account. Gravv already processes more than $3 billion a year for over a thousand businesses in more than 100 countries, and today the business page doesn't say so.

I'd publish one numbers page with each figure, its definition and an "as of" date, and point every page, ad and press release at it. For a regulated product, that page does trust work for customers, partners and journalists at once.

How I'd test it in the first 30 days

I'd publish the numbers page first, so every variant cites the same figures. Then I'd run the current rotating headline against "Send money home. Build your US credit." on US diaspora paid traffic and the homepage for two weeks, split by source, and measure install starts and first transfers. The UK rate-led hero would run the same way on UK traffic. On the business side, the payouts page would go first with one named customer, measured on qualified demo requests against today's business page.

If the plain credit line wins, it becomes the lead for paid creative and the App Store listing the same week. If it loses, that tells us the credit claim needs its proof page before it can lead.

Method and limits

I read the pages from Lagos on 5 October 2026 and took the screenshots on this page. I checked the headline by sampling it every half second for twelve seconds, which is how I found the six rotating verbs. I haven't seen Kredete's analytics, research or any internal positioning work, so this is a starting draft built from the outside. Which product leads in each market should come from Kredete's own retention and margin data, and that's the first thing I'd ask for.
