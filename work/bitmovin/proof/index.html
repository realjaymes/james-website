Encoder V3 general availability: a launch brief

Encoder V3's general release: one position, one number per buyer

James Praise · Product marketing and GTM

The launch brief

Intro

Your Encoder Version 3 announcement says general availability for VOD production is coming over the next month. A general release is the moment a launch reaches buyers who did not join the beta, so the story has to be simple enough for them.

I wrote this brief from outside, using your public pages as they stood on 19 November 2026. It is a first draft to test against your sales calls and your product team's plans.

The position

One line. Encoder V3 gives you the same picture with nearly a third fewer bits, and cuts the price of AV1 in half for managed cloud customers.

Why this line. The bits are what your customers pay to deliver, every time a viewer presses play. AV1 is the codec that saves the most, and price was the main reason to wait. V3 answers both in one sentence a finance lead and an engineer can both repeat.

One claim, three pillars, one number each. Every surface for the launch draws from this table.

Where the other numbers go. The 15.5% for the upgraded HEVC codec, the 17.6% on a static ladder and the 25% compute saving all stay true. They belong in the method section and the docs, where an engineer checking the claim will look for them.

One number per page

What I found. The same launch leads with a different figure depending on where a buyer meets it. Each figure is true for a different codec or kind of content. A buyer who sees two of them has no way to tell which one applies to their library.

WhereHeadlineWhat it measures
Homepage news card, title"up to 35% fewer bits"AV1 Per-Title on sports content only
Homepage news card, image30.6% and 15.5%AV1 across all content, and the upgraded HEVC codec

A second fix before release. In the V3 blog's questions section, "Why are Per-Title numbers larger for AV1?" repeats the answer to the question before it, word for word. An engineer checking the 30.6% looks exactly there for the reason.

What I'd do. Lead every general surface with 30.6%, keep 35% for sports pages and talks, and fix the repeated answer before the general release.

The gap this fills. Your V3 material speaks well to the engineer. The finance lead is the reader with nothing written for them yet, and that person often decides when a switch happens.

The agent channel

What I found. Your homepage asks developers to let their AI get to know Bitmovin. That is ahead of most of your competitors. In your own Video Developer Report, 46% of respondents use AI tools every day, so an agent's answer is becoming part of how engineers choose.

The skill package was downloaded 97 times from 19 October to 17 November, the month after the V3 announcement. Its V3 entry gives no numbers, and the GitHub repository description still says it covers only the web player.

What I'd do. Update the skill and the repository description in the same hour as the release post, with the V3 numbers and how to turn V3 on. Then count installs as a launch metric. Each month, ask the main coding agents how to cut AV1 encoding cost, and record whether they name Bitmovin.

Launch plan

Two weeks before release. Agree the message house with product, engineering and sales. Fix the homepage card and the repeated answer. Write the business case and the calculator. Pre-brief analysts and the outside testers who reviewed V3. Give sales a talk track for the competitor comparison in your blog, where the other encoder needed 58.5% more bitrate.

Release week. Publish the general availability post and update the skill the same hour. Email your VOD accounts with a savings estimate at their own volume. Run a live engineering session that walks through the test method. Share the launch from Reinhard's and Stefan's own LinkedIn accounts, in their words.

The first 60 days. Publish two customer stories, one of them from sports. Report AV1's share of encoded minutes monthly. Build a question about codec choice into the next Video Developer Report, and turn the results into a conference talk.

How I'd measure it

  • Accounts running V3 in production, against a target set with sales.
  • AV1's share of encoded minutes, the clearest sign the price cut worked.
  • New trial accounts that encode with V3 in their free 2,000 minutes.
  • Pipeline from deals where V3 came up.
  • Skill installs and signups that start from an agent.

One guardrail sits beside them. Quality complaints after the switch should stay flat, because a launch that wins on cost cannot afford to lose on picture.
