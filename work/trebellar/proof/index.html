A go-to-market teardown for Trebellar

A corporate real estate buyer can't compare Trebellar, price it or read a case study before booking a demo.

James Praise · Positioning, Messaging and Go-to-Market

I wrote this from Trebellar's public pages on October 14, 2026: the homepage, the blog, the About page, the security page, the demo page, the sitemap and the Ashby jobs board. I have not seen the product, a demo or a sales call, so everything here describes what a buyer sees before talking to anyone at Trebellar.

1. What already works

The three things below are done well, and I would leave them alone.

  • The first screen of the homepage names the job. Trebellar unifies a team's corporate real estate data and puts AI agents to work planning, analyzing and executing.
  • The security page answers a buyer's information technology (IT) review in advance. It states System and Organization Controls (SOC) 2 Type II, certified since July 2024, and gives typical setup times of two to three weeks for a manual comma-separated values (CSV) upload and two to four weeks for a direct warehouse connection.
  • The demo page promises a reply within 20 minutes on business days, and the site publishes an llms.txt file written for AI assistants.

trebellar.com's first screen on October 14, 2026. The navigation holds Platform, About, Blog, Log In and Request Demo. The four logos under the video are Meta, Uber, Merck and Cohesity.

2. What a buyer can't find

The sitemap lists 21 pages. Seven are blog posts. The rest are the homepage, six product and platform pages, the blog index, About, Security, Careers, the demo form and two legal pages. None is a comparison page, a pricing page or a customer story, and trebellar.com/pricing returns a not-found page.

Nobody on the page says how Trebellar differs from the alternatives

The seven posts all argue for Trebellar's approach, and the three co-founders wrote every one. I searched them for Eptura, Serraview, VergeSense, MRI, Archibus, Visual Lease, Mapiq, JLL, CBRE, Workplacer and Planon and found none. A buyer who already has two or three platforms on a list learns nothing from the site about how Trebellar compares.

Nobody on the page says what it costs or what a first project involves

The navigation and footer have no pricing page. The page I would add has no price list. It explains what drives the cost and what a pilot involves, which a buyer needs before spending a meeting on it. Eptura, a platform the assistant names for the same questions below, puts Pricing in its top navigation, and that link opens a plans page that lists Serraview by Eptura.

Nobody on the page shows what a customer did

The homepage shows four logos, and the Series A post thanks Meta, Uber, Merck, Cohesity "and others". The homepage carousel holds four quotes credited to Andrew P., Jeremy K., Mark G. and Laura D. The platform page and the agents page each carry one quote credited to a name and a title, a VP of Global Workplace Services and a Global Head of Places. No quote names a company, so a buyer cannot tell whether any of them works at one of the four logo companies, and no page says what any customer did with Trebellar or what changed.

No open role owns these pages

The Ashby board lists five roles: a Forward Deployed Engineer, a Senior Software Engineer, a Customer Success Manager, a Forward Deployed Expert and a Sales Engineer. Three sit under Sales & Marketing, and none of the five is a marketing role. The careers page carries a search description that says Trebellar is hiring marketers, and the board has no marketing posting. I read all three Sales & Marketing descriptions. The Sales Engineer owns the demo environment, proof of concept, data readiness audit and security reviews. The Customer Success Manager works with accounts after the contract is signed. The Forward Deployed Expert works with customers after implementation. All three start at the demo or later, and none writes the pages a buyer reads before it.

The second is that Workplacer, an AI-native platform the assistant called promising, was named in the first answer. A new name can get into these answers, and it gets there with pages that answer the question being asked.

4. The three pages I'd publish first

The order follows what a buyer asks in turn: who else is on the shortlist, what it costs, and who has already done it. Each page is written for the person who has to defend the purchase to a chief financial officer and a head of IT. Everything below is an outline. The facts I could verify are filled in with their source, and everything else is marked for Trebellar to supply.

A comparison page

One hub page, with a short page per platform added later. The opening paragraph would read:

If you are choosing a platform for your corporate real estate portfolio, you are probably weighing Trebellar against a planning suite such as Eptura Serraview and an occupancy analytics tool such as VergeSense Meridian. This page sets out how each describes itself, what to ask every vendor, and where Trebellar is a worse fit.

Headlines to test, in the order I'd try them:

Trebellar compared with Eptura Serraview and VergeSense Meridian The version that matches how a buyer types the question.
How to choose a corporate real estate platform: six things to ask every vendor A buying guide that ranks for the question before the buyer has named a vendor.
Trebellar and the other platforms on your shortlist The plainest version, with no vendor names in the headline.

Beside that table, the page sets out the shortlist using each vendor's own description. Serraview describes right-sizing a portfolio with real-time workplace insights. Envision describes dashboards that bring workplace and real estate data into a single view. Meridian describes turning real-time occupancy signals into forecasts. I have not written any claim about a competitor's weakness, because I can't verify one from outside.

A page on how pricing works

No number on it. It answers the five things a buyer needs before a second meeting:

  • What drives the cost. Number of sites, users, data sources or agents. Trebellar to supply.
  • What a first project includes. The data the buyer sends, the weeks involved and what they have at the end. The setup times on the security page are the starting point.
  • Who has to be involved. Workplace, finance, IT and security, and the hours each is asked for.
  • What is not included. Stated plainly, so the buyer does not find it in the contract.
  • What happens next. The demo, then a proof of concept, then a contract, with the usual length of each.

The Sales Engineer job description already names the proof of concept and the data readiness audit as parts of the sales process, so most of this page describes steps your team has defined and a buyer has not been shown.

I would start with whichever of Meta, Uber, Merck and Cohesity will go on record. If none will yet, I would start with the people already quoted on the site, add their company, title and one number each, and publish those as short stories. The homepage quote about saving $10 million for every 1% better decision is a strong claim, and a buyer will want the story behind it.

5. How to know whether any of this is right

I built this from the outside and I don't have your first-call data, so treat the three pages as hypotheses. Four ways to test them, in order of speed.

List the questions your first calls already raise

Ask Adam and the account executives for the questions that come up on every first call. Each repeated question is a section of one of the three pages, and it is the most reliable input available.

Ask who else the buyer is evaluating, in the demo form

The demo form has an open field labelled "Anything we should know?". A required line asking which other platforms the buyer is weighing tells you which comparison to write first, from your own pipeline.

Rerun the three questions every month

Record which platforms are named and which pages are cited. A comparison page earns its place when Trebellar starts appearing in the answer to the alternatives question.

Compare buyers who read the pages with buyers who did not

Track demo-to-proof-of-concept conversion for visitors who viewed a comparison, pricing or customer page against those who did not. Volume is low at this stage, so read it as direction until there are enough demos to be sure.

What I couldn't check

I have not seen the product, a demo or a sales call, so I can't say which of these pages would move a deal. The assistant's answers come from one run on October 14, 2026 and change from run to run, so the useful number is the one you collect monthly. I could not load MRI Software's page, so I report only that the assistant named it. I have not seen Trebellar's analytics or its demo requests, which is where the real answer to which comparison comes first is sitting.
