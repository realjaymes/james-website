A planning model for Supabase

Planning Supabase's GTM Around What Developers Need Next

James Praise · Marketing and GTM Operator

This is built from public information read on 16 November 2026: your pricing page, the job posting and what your team has said publicly. I can't see your usage data, pipeline or headcount plan, so every threshold below is a starting guess to test against your data, and anything that needs your numbers is marked [proof needed]. PLS means product-led sales: sales outreach triggered by what an account does in the product.

The model

Where I'd start

The GTM, Strategic Planning posting asks for coverage models and segmentation "that reflect how developers actually buy." When Roman Gruhn announced he was joining, he named new motions "for enterprise, new customers, partners, and platforms." So I started with one question: what tells you a developer account is about to need a person?

The step that decides coverage

Your pricing page answers most of it. Pro starts at $25 a month. Team starts at $599, about 24 times more, and what it adds is mostly security and access control: SOC 2 and ISO 27001, single sign-on to the dashboard, read-only and project-scoped roles, and HIPAA as a paid add-on.

Usage alone rarely moves a team across that step, because Pro already scales by usage. A security review, a compliance deadline or a growing team does. So I'd segment on the need that triggers the next plan, and treat company size as a tiebreaker.

Five segments

SegmentHow an account entersWho covers it
BuildersFree or Pro, one or two projects, no team or security signalsThe product and lifecycle email, no person

Each threshold is a guess to test [proof needed]. The useful part is that every account carries one segment and one reason, so a rep can see why it is in their queue.

Capacity from signal volume

For segments that move fast, I'd size the team from how many accounts enter each month, not from a list of named accounts. The sum is simple: accounts entering a segment each month, times the hours each one needs, divided by the selling hours one rep has in a month.

With made-up inputs, 400 Scaling accounts a month at an hour and a half each, against 100 selling hours per rep, needs six reps. Your real entry rates set the hiring plan [proof needed].

Territories inside segments

Geography still matters, but only inside the segments that get a person. You work across 60+ countries, so I'd split Security-ready and Strategic into time zone pods for the Americas, Europe and Africa, and Asia Pacific. A rep then covers accounts awake during their working day. Strategic accounts stay named, and Builders and Scaling stay pooled worldwide.

One source of truth

Usage lives in BigQuery and the sales team works in HubSpot. I'd write the segment logic once, as a model in BigQuery that outputs each organization's segment, the reason and the date it changed. Hightouch syncs those fields to the HubSpot company record, and Hex holds the planning views. Sales, finance and talent acquisition then plan from the same numbers.

What I'd track

Four numbers each month: how many accounts move from Scaling or Security-ready to a paid upgrade, the time from a signal to the first human touch, pipeline and win rate by segment, and the share of each rep's capacity in use. I'd review thresholds quarterly and move any that send reps accounts that never convert.

The first 90 days

Days 1 to 30: agree the definitions

I'd map how HubSpot and BigQuery describe an account today, sit with sales, success and finance to agree what each segment means, and backtest the thresholds against the last year of upgrades.

Days 31 to 60: ship the model

With GTM Engineering, I'd put the segment model into production, sync it to HubSpot and build the first coverage and capacity views in Hex.

Days 61 to 90: plan the next year

I'd turn entry rates into next year's targets and headcount by segment and pod, then agree the hiring order with talent acquisition and finance.

Appendix

A first segment query

This is how the first version of the segment model could look. The table and column names are placeholders, because I haven't seen your warehouse.

-- One row per organization: its segment, the reason, and when it was computed with org_signals as ( select o.org_id, o.plan, -- free, pro, team, enterprise count(distinct p.project_id) as projects, max(p.compute_size_rank) as max_compute_rank, -- micro = 1, large = 4, 16xl = 10 max(cast(o.spend_cap_enabled as int64)) as spend_cap_on, count(distinct m.member_id) as members, countif(v.page in ('soc2','hipaa','sso') and v.viewed_at >= date_sub(current_date(), interval 30 day)) as security_views, sum(b.mrr_usd) as mrr_usd from orgs o left join projects p using (org_id) left join org_members m using (org_id) left join page_views v using (org_id) left join billing b using (org_id) group by 1, 2 ) select org_id, case when is_platform_partner(org_id) then 'platforms_partners' when plan = 'enterprise' or mrr_usd >= 5000 then 'strategic' when security_views > 0 or members >= 5 then 'security_ready' when max_compute_rank >= 4 or spend_cap_on = 0 or projects >= 3 then 'scaling' else 'builders' end as segment, current_timestamp() as computed_at from org_signals;

In production each rule would also write its reason, so the HubSpot record says "five or more members" rather than only "Security-ready." The $5,000 strategic line is a placeholder.
