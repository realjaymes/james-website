Three things I found, with the receipts

I reviewed Softr the way I review any product before I write about it, stage by stage, with a screenshot at every step. These three carry the most weight for a self-serve growth role.

The attribution instrument cannot see your loudest channel Every user who arrives through a founder post gets filed as Other, or picks the nearest wrong answer. A wrong answer is worse than a blank one, because it moves budget.
The Co-Builder's questions are the activation moment, not the generation Before building anything it asked three things my prompt never mentioned: whether attendees sign in, whether feedback ties to specific events, and what rating scale to use. The app was live with sample data in under two minutes, and nothing happens with that moment today.
Every free and Basic app published is already a branded surface Branding removal unlocks at Pro, $99 a month, not at Basic. Webflow turned the identical surface into Made in Webflow, a public gallery that ranks and converts. Softr has the badge and not the gallery.

All eleven findings, with the rest of the screenshots, sit in the brief.

The three experiments I'd run first

I scored the backlog on ICEEE: Impact, Confidence, and three separate Effort scores for engineering, marketing and everything else. The effort scores are inverted, so ten means almost no build and one means a large one. That keeps a cheap, certain, medium-impact test ahead of an expensive moonshot.

What I would do in the first thirty days

This backlog was built from the outside, which is exactly why I would not run it as written on day one. I would spend the first month replacing my assumptions with your data.

  1. I would learn the funnel before I touch it. I want to know how Softr defines an activated user today, where the self-serve funnel actually leaks between signup and a published app, and which of these tests someone has already run.
  2. I would ship the attribution fix in week one. It is the smallest build on the list and every later channel decision reads from that instrument, so it goes first regardless of what else I learn.
  3. I would answer the four open questions with internal data. Those are the ones about the template gallery, the Partner programme, how MCP is being taken to market, and whether the influencer audience overlaps the founder-led one. I wrote them as questions rather than proposals because guessing at them would waste your time.
  4. I would take the share loop to Product as a joint experiment. It is a product build, not a campaign, so it needs an engineering partner and a slot, not a marketing brief.
  5. I would rebuild this backlog from what I find. The scores here are honest, and they are also built on public evidence only. The version that matters is the one scored against your retention curves and your credit consumption data.
