A plan for ClassDojo

Sparks for Families Outside the Network: A First Creator Test

James Praise · Growth, paid social and AI systems

This plan uses only public sources: your job posting, your product pages and the Meta Ad Library, all read on 24 November 2026. I cannot see your trial, retention or cost data, so anything only your numbers can settle is marked [proof needed].

The plan

What Tutor already shows

You already win parents who have never heard of ClassDojo. In the Meta Ad Library, about nine partnership ads tagged #classdojopartner were running in the US. Teacher and parent creators such as tionnateaches and Brianna Merritt post them, and ClassDojo pays to show them.

Every one I opened was for Dojo Tutor. They send parents to tutor.classdojo.com, a site of its own, with a $5 first class as the offer. So the hard parts are already solved inside the company: briefing creators, running partnership ads and landing a parent who arrives cold.

Searches for "Dojo Sparks" and "Dojo Islands" returned no active ads.

Where Sparks loses a cold parent

Take a parent of a 4 year old who sees a reading creator talk about Sparks. Three things get in the way.

The page says it isn't for them.

The Sparks pricing page says access is "available exclusively to ClassDojo families through the ClassDojo app". A parent with no school connection reads that as a closed door.

The offer changes between sections.

The Sparks page says "30 day trial, money back guaranteed" in one place and "try it free for 7 days" in another. A test can't tell you what worked if the offer isn't fixed.

There is nothing to believe yet.

The page has three parent quotes and no outcome. A stranger has no reason to trust a reading app over the free ones.

This matters more for Sparks than for any other product, because Sparks starts at age 3. Most 3 and 4 year olds are not in a K-12 classroom, so their parents are not on ClassDojo through a teacher. They are the families this role exists to reach.

A front door for outside families

I would copy the Tutor pattern before spending on traffic. One landing page for Sparks that works for a parent with no school connection, one trial offer used on every page, and the shortest path from a creator's post to a child reading.

Any parent can start Sparks. No school account needed. Free for 7 days, then $64.99 a year.

That line is a placeholder, and it depends on product rules I can't see. ClassDojo Plus already works for kids who "don't use ClassDojo at school", so the idea has a precedent. Whether a parent can start Sparks without connecting to a school is [proof needed].

I would add two or three outcome lines from your own Sparks data, for example how many sounds a child practises in the first week. Those are [proof needed].

The number that decides it

Trial starts look good in Ads Manager and don't pay the bills. I would judge every creator on cost per paying family, from Stripe and the app stores, against what a family is worth in year one.

Stop and scale rules

WhenRule
Day 14Cut any creator whose cost per trial is above the break-even line and whose trials have not started converting.
Day 21Once the first trials end, rank creators on cost per paying family. Double the budget on the top three.
WeeklyCheck trials reported by Meta and TikTok against Stripe and the app stores. Act on the store numbers.
Day 45If three or more creators beat the line, brief 20 more who look like them, and consider an agency to find and manage them.

The weekly check comes from my own work. On my products, Meta's pixel was crediting one product's sales to another, and I only found it by checking against our sales records. With a free trial, platforms will over-report.

Appendix

Creator brief

Each creator would get one page. It explains what Sparks is in one sentence, shows a child using it, and lists what to film: the first session, a moment the coach helps, and the parent's honest reaction. It also covers what not to say (no learning promises we can't back up), the disclosure rules, and the usage rights that let ClassDojo run the post as an ad for 60 days.

Islands, second

Dojo Islands is free, and inviting friends over is part of the game. Its fastest growth is probably the friend invite, which sits close to product and to network growth. I would bring a referral test to the product team after the Sparks test is running, and keep paid spend on Sparks, where the price makes the maths clear.

What I need from you

Sparks trial-to-paid rate, month-two and month-six retention, the split between annual and monthly plans, current cost per paying family, and the share of Sparks families who join with no school connection. I would also want to hear what the Tutor team learned from its creator ads, and whether a parent can start Sparks without a school account.
