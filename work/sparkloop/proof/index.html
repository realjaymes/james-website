The case study

The operator's story

Welcome Surveys collect data on new subscribers inside SparkLoop's signup flow. A feature like that sells on one believable customer story, so I built the page around one operator and his numbers.

He already grows for free. He spends about $2.50 to acquire a subscriber on Meta and earns about $2.60 back through SparkLoop's paid recommendations. Growth was never his problem. His old survey topped out near 30% completion, so about 70% of the subscribers he paid for told him nothing.

Completion doubledFrom about 30% to 65% or more. The form shows the welcome offers a subscriber unlocks by finishing, so more of them finish.
No extra questionsAI enrichment fills in role, company size and location. He asks three or four quick questions and gets the rest for free.
Sponsors surface on their ownAbout 1 to 2% of new subscribers get flagged as a strong sponsor fit. He closes roughly 1 in 10. At that rate he's on track for $50,000 to $60,000 a year.

The page

The case study page is modeled on SparkLoop's own customer stories, with a stronger cold open. Most visitors arrive from an ad and have never heard of the operator, so the headline speaks to the reader's own list first.

Grow your newsletter for free. Then let it find your sponsors.

The campaign

The $10K plan

The goal is early-access signups from professional newsletter operators, judged on cost per signup. Three hypotheses drive it.

ChannelProfessional newsletter operators can be reached on LinkedIn and will convert there, so LinkedIn is the main paid channel.
Angle"Grow, then monetize" beats a feature list.
SourceThe operator's own audience and voice convert cheaper than cold ads, because operators trust operators.

Budget and waves

LineBudgetWhat it buys
LinkedIn ads$6,000 to $6,500Traffic to the case study page
Creative production$1,500Carousels, the operator's screen recording, static ads
Operator amplification$1,000His list send, his posts and a small cross-promotion
Test reserve$1,000An X test and retargeting of page visitors who didn't sign up

Wave 1 spends $2,000 over one week across all five hooks and ranks them by cost per signup. Wave 2 puts $4,000 behind the one or two winners, adds fresh hooks in the winning format and layers on retargeting. The operator's posts and list send run the whole time at no ad cost. The weekly report follows the funnel from click-through to page conversion to cost per signup, and losing angles get cut early.
