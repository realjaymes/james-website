A lifecycle and monetization plan for FIFU

Your PRO wall says nothing at the exact moment someone is ready to pay you.

James Praise · Marketing and GTM Operator

I did not review your marketing site for this. Your WordPress.org listing links to a live test site with the free version installed, so I went through the product itself as a free user would: every settings tab, the Cloud add-on, the WordPress Plugins list page, and the deactivation flow. The screenshots below are from that session.

1. The free flow works exactly as promised

I want to start here because it matters for what follows. Your Help tab walks a new user through the entire free flow in two steps: copy an image URL, paste it into the Featured Image field. I created a real post and did it myself.

Your Help tab, which explains the whole free flow in two steps.

The field itself, "Image URL or Keywords" with a Preview button. Clean, and it does exactly what it says.

There is no friction anywhere in this path. Whatever the free-to-paid gap is, it is not here.

2. The PRO wall is completely silent

This is the finding. Every settings tab marks PRO-gated sections with a small yellow badge, so free users can see that PRO features exist.

The PRO badge on the Image tab. It identifies. It does not explain what upgrading gets you, and it does not make it easy to act.

Then there are the toggles. "Featured video" on the Video tab is marked PRO and looks fully interactive.

I clicked this toggle. Nothing happened. No popup, no message, no link, no visual change at all. The same on every other PRO toggle I tried.

At the precise moment a free user reaches for a PRO feature, which is the moment they are most ready to pay you, they get silence instead of an answer. Sixty thousand free installs sit behind that wall.

The gating is also finer than tab-level, which matters for any fix.

On the Automatic tab, "Auto set featured media from post content" is free, but "Run it for all posts" in the same panel is PRO. A fix has to work at this level, not just gate whole tabs.

3. You already know how to do this. Twice.

Two things on your own surfaces prove the capability is there and simply has not been pointed at the PRO license.

Your Developers tab lists every function in a clean table with an explicit PRO column. Scannable, unambiguous, genuinely well done. It just lives in a reference table instead of at the moment of use.

FIFU Cloud, your separate add-on, has a dedicated admin page with a working Account tab: an email field and a Sign up button, right inside WordPress. That is a real in-app conversion flow. It has never been applied to the core PRO license.

There is also an "Upgrade to PRO" link already in the product, but it is in the wrong place.

The link sits on the standard WordPress Plugins list page, not inside FIFU's settings where people actually hit the wall. It opens fifu.app's homepage in a new tab. Not a pricing page, and not a page explaining what PRO unlocks.

4. What the upgrade messaging could actually say

You asked specifically about upgrade messaging, so here is copy rather than a recommendation that copy should exist. These are first drafts, not final:

On the Featured Video toggle "Featured Video is a PRO feature. Set a YouTube, Vimeo, or direct video URL as your featured media, no code required. See what PRO unlocks."
On the Featured Slider toggle "Featured Slider is a PRO feature. Turn your featured media into a swipeable gallery, built for real estate, portfolios, and product listings. See what PRO unlocks."

Both name the specific feature, say what it does in one line, and link somewhere real instead of the homepage. That is the pattern worth repeating everywhere: specific, tied to what the person just tried to do.

5. The intent signal you are already collecting

Deactivating FIFU triggers a modal asking why, promising that "the developer will respond within 8 hours", with example prompts including "Works well, but I would need a new or PRO feature..."

Your deactivation survey. People selecting that option are telling you, in their own words, that they wanted a PRO feature and did not get it.

That is a real intent-capture moment you have already built. What I could not confirm from outside your account is whether the response is tied to an email address anywhere, or what "the developer will respond" actually routes to. If it is tied to an email, that is a list of people who already told you exactly what they wanted, and a short, low-pressure follow-up to that group is close to a free win.

A broader sequence needs email capture first, which I could not find anywhere on the free plugin. Only Cloud has an opt-in today. If capture gets added, a light three-email structure would do: something practical and useful in the first few days with no pitch at all, a message triggered the moment someone hits a PRO wall that names the exact feature they tried, and a softer close built around a real use case for anyone still on free.

This should not wait for the in-app fix to ship. They reinforce each other. Someone who ignores a prompt in the plugin today may still convert from a well-timed email next week.

6. How you would know if it worked

The in-app fix has a clean before and after, because the current state is zero. Today a PRO toggle click produces nothing measurable at all. Once a prompt exists, you can count prompt views by feature, clicks through to pricing, and conversions attributed to each specific wall.

That last one is the number that matters, because it tells you which PRO features people actually want, as opposed to which ones you assumed they wanted. My guess is that the answer will surprise you, and I would rather you had the data than my guess.

7. What I could not check

I went through the plugin as a free user on your own public test site, so the product findings above are first-hand. Three things stayed out of reach.

I could not see whether the deactivation survey captures an email, which is the single biggest determinant of whether the easiest win here is available. I could not see your PRO conversion rate or revenue, so nothing above is sized. And I did not test the PRO version itself, so I am describing the free experience and the wall, not what sits behind it.
