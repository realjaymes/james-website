Everything here uses public material only: Shape's website as it appeared on 17 September 2026 (screenshots below), Shape's published prices, and published benchmark studies linked where they are used. Part 1: Working back from the number

The short version

Thirty held demos a month needs about 66 demo requests if a qualified request can book a meeting on the spot, and about 146 if it can't. At the same channel costs that's the difference between roughly $14,400 and $32,100 a month in media. So the first thing I'd fix is the path from form to calendar, before adding budget.

Shape can also afford more per demo than most software companies. Professional starts at $1,650 a month billed annually, about $19,800 a year per account. Worked through the win rates below, a held demo is worth $3,960 to $4,950 in first-year revenue, which puts a sensible media ceiling at $1,320 to $1,650 per held demo. My base plan lands near $480.

What Shape can pay per demo

Most demand gen plans borrow a cost-per-lead benchmark. Shape doesn't need to, because it publishes its prices. Professional costs $1,650 a month billed annually and Professional Plus $2,475, so the entry account is worth about $19,800 a year, and a Professional Plus account about $29,700.

Published B2B software win rates sit around 20% to 30%, with enterprise opportunities closing near 31% in The Digital Bloom's compilation of 40+ benchmark studies. At a 20% to 25% win rate, each held demo is worth $3,960 to $4,950 in first-year revenue on the Professional plan alone. If media spend is held to a third of that first-year value, leaving room for sales cost and margin, Shape can pay $1,320 to $1,650 per held demo.

That ceiling is useful in two ways. It tells the Head of Marketing how hard paid can be pushed before it stops paying back, and it gives LinkedIn, the most expensive channel, room to cost two or three times more than I've assumed and still work.

Shape Aware and the weekly scorecard

The posting describes Shape Aware as people who have visited the website or engaged with content or ads. To make it a number the team can watch, I'd define it in HubSpot as a contact or company with at least one of these in the last 90 days: a tracked visit to pricing, the demo page or a resource; an ad engagement synced from LinkedIn or Meta; or a download or webinar registration. I'd count it by account as well as by person, because one engaged commercial manager at Kier matters more than ten students reading a blog post.

Part 2: The path to a booked demo

Where the first gains are

I walked the path a commercial manager would take on 17 September 2026: homepage, pricing, demo page and resources. These are the fixes I'd make first, ranked by how much each should move held demos.

1. Let a qualified buyer book on the spot

The demo page asks for first name, last name, work email, phone, job function, project type, main challenge and a description of the project, then a "Schedule a Demo" button. I couldn't see a calendar before submitting. If a person books the call after that, this is the stage the Chili Piper numbers point at. HubSpot, which Shape already runs, has a meetings scheduler that can show a calendar on the thank-you page, routed by the job function and project type fields the form already collects.

Shape's demo page, 17 September 2026.

I'd keep the qualifying fields, because they route the buyer to the right person, and test making the project description optional or moving it to the booking step.

2. Move the money proof up

Shape's strongest proof is a customer who recovered $300,000, 4.5% of total project value. It sits below the form on the demo page. The homepage hero already says "Capture every change on site and recover all your costs." That claim would carry more weight with the $300,000 figure beside it, above the fold.

The $300,000 recovery proof sits under the form on the demo page.

3. Turn "your recovery potential" into something a buyer can see first

The demo page promises buyers will leave "knowing the ROI" and their "recovery potential." A short estimate before the call (project value and number of changes in, estimated unrecovered cost against the subscription out) would give paid traffic a reason to engage before they're ready for a meeting, and feed Shape Aware. I'd build it with Shape's commercial team so the unrecovered-change percentage comes from Shape's own project data rather than a borrowed industry figure.

4. Fix the webinar card that's five months out of date

The only webinar on the resources center, "Fixing Field-to-Office Communication on Major Projects", is dated 15 April 2026 and still shows as upcoming. I'd replace it with the on-demand recording behind a short form and the date of the next session. A running monthly series is the cheapest part of the second engine to start.

Shape's resources center: a gated report, templates and product updates, with no webinar hub.

5. Get reviews on the sites buyers check

Shape has tier-one logos like Balfour Beatty and Kier, yet Capterra shows 0 reviews. A commercial buyer who is about to trust Shape with claims evidence will look. A review request to current users, timed after a recorded recovery, would get Capterra and G2 into double figures within a quarter and give paid campaigns a third-party rating to show.

6. Test UK contract language in UK ads

The site speaks in US terms ("change order," "GC," and a Change Order Log (US Version) template), and most customer logos are UK firms. That may be deliberate for a US push. For UK commercial managers, I'd test ads and landing page copy that use "variations" and "compensation events" against the current wording, and let the cost per held demo decide.

The handoff specification

The posting asks for sales to be notified "instantly and properly every time one books." This is how I'd set it up in HubSpot:

  • Routing: a qualified submission shows the calendar of the matching sales development representative (SDR) on the thank-you page, chosen by job function, project type and whether the company is on the GTM Engineer's target list. Disqualified submissions (students, suppliers, competitors) get a resource and no calendar.
  • Alert: a Slack or Teams message and a HubSpot task the moment a meeting books, carrying name, company, role, project type, main challenge, the project description, pages viewed, the ad or campaign that brought them, and whether the account is on the target list.
  • Speed rule: any qualified request that doesn't book gets a call or personal email from an SDR within five working minutes, and the alert escalates to the sales manager if nobody claims it in 15.
  • No-show rule: an automatic rebooking email within the hour, then an SDR call the same day.
  • Feedback: sales marks each held demo as qualified or not in HubSpot within 24 hours, with a reason. That field feeds the offline conversions and the weekly lead-quality review with the SDR team.

Part 3: The plan

The first 90 days

Weeks 1–4: See the funnel and fix the handoff

  • Set up HubSpot lifecycle stages and the Shape Aware definition, audit campaign tracking, and publish the first weekly scorecard by the end of week 2, even with gaps.
  • Replace my show rate, booking rate and cost assumptions with Shape's last 90 days of data.
  • Put instant booking, routing and the alert live, and agree the qualified-demo definition with sales.
  • Swap the stale webinar card for the recording and move the $300,000 proof up the homepage.

Weeks 5–8: Scale paid on what the data says

  • Run Google Search on change, claims and site diary intent, plus competitor terms, with offline conversion imports.
  • Run LinkedIn against the target account list by role (commercial, project and construction managers), with the Evidencing Field Change report and a direct demo offer as separate tests.
  • Start retargeting Shape Aware audiences, and brief content and design on two ad angles a week based on cost per held demo.
  • Report weekly against 30 held demos, with the gap explained by stage.

Weeks 9–12: Build the second engine

  • Run a monthly webinar for commercial managers on evidencing site change, with the recording as an always-on lead magnet.
  • Run the first "lunch and learn" sessions for five to ten named accounts with sales, chosen from the GTM Engineer's intent signals.
  • Start the review drive and the UK language test.
  • Plan 2027 events with real budget attached: Futurebuild and UK Construction Week London at ExCeL, 11–13 May 2027, and Digital Construction Week at ExCeL, 23–24 June 2027, each with a pre-booked meeting target and a follow-up plan agreed with sales before sign-off.

Assumptions to replace with Shape's data

I've kept these visible because they carry the plan, and I don't have Shape's internal numbers:

  • Show rate of 80%. Replace with HubSpot meeting outcomes.
  • LinkedIn at $300, retargeting at $150 and webinars at $125 per demo request. Replace with the first four weeks of spend.
  • Search click-to-request rate of 3%. Set below the published Business Services average on purpose. Replace with Shape's own Google Ads data if it exists.
  • Win rate of 20% to 25% and the Professional price as the account value. Replace with Shape's real close rate and average contract value, which may be higher if most customers are on Professional Plus or run several projects.
  • The 30/40/18/12 channel mix. A starting split, rebalanced every two weeks toward the lowest cost per held demo.
  • No post-submission booking step today. I didn't submit the form, so this is the first thing I'd check with the team.
