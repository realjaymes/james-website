Part 1 · Tier 1, the printed mail

Each named account gets a map of its own website, with a code that leads to a call

What the code does

It opens a private page made for that university, with the full write-up of what the map found and a link to book 15 minutes with Alex. Each scan sends Alex a Slack alert, and he calls that day.

The 14 days

Day 0: an email and a LinkedIn connection from Alex. Day 2: Alex calls to check who should receive the mail. Day 4: it arrives. Day 6: an email with the page link. Day 9: a second call. Day 14: a last email. About £15 an account, £750 for all 50 (my estimate).

Part 2 · What the numbers say

Half the people open the emails, and almost nobody replies with interest

50%+ openedDeliverability is above 80/100, so the emails arrive. Some opens are Apple Mail loading images by itself
0.7% repliedAbout 21 replies of any kind per 3,000. A working campaign gets about 20 interested ones, the target in your own outbound package
5% positive5% of the 21 replies: about 1 interested person per 3,000
10%+ clickedAbout 300 clicks on emails with no links. That's security software, not people

Low replies with high opens point to the offer and the message. So few interested replies point to the wrong people being emailed. I'd switch click tracking off: the number can't be trusted, and tracked links hurt delivery.

Part 2 · Where it breaks

The emails ask dealers to replace software their whole sales floor runs on

The offer doesn't fit the buyerDealers run a dealer management system, such as CDK or Reynolds and Reynolds, with a dealer CRM wired into it. Replacing that by cold email is close to impossible.
Everyone gets the same emailOwners, IT, parts and fleet staff all get one message. 50 to 1,000 staff mixes single stores with groups that own many.
No reason to reply nowNothing says why this week. "A free audit call" asks a stranger for 30 minutes before giving anything.
The message is about MattEmail 1 opens "I'm Matt, Founder & CEO" and lists HubSpot features any industry could get.
The follow-ups add nothingVersions A and B differ by one sentence, so the test can't show what works.
Two emails may go out unsignedEmails 2A and 2B use {{Signature}} where the rest use {{signature}}. Many tools treat those as different fields.

Part 2 · Who to target instead

Sell HubSpot next to the dealer CRM, to the people who feel what it leaves undone

Job titles

  • Owner, Dealer Principal, General Manager
  • Service Director and Service Manager
  • Sales managers and the team that answers web leads and books visits
  • Marketing Director, at dealer groups only
  • Off the list: IT, parts, fleet, analytics

Size, location, who to leave out

  • Separate campaigns for dealer groups and single stores
  • US first. Canada later, as its own campaign under Canada's anti-spam law
  • Leave out dealers that already pay a HubSpot agency

Signals

  • HubSpot's tracking code already on the website
  • A store just bought by a dealer group. More than 600 were sold in 2025 (Haig Partners)
  • A new General Manager or Service Director
  • Job posts for sales or service staff
  • Google reviews complaining about service updates

Part 2 · What to say instead

The new emails lead with the service customers dealers are losing

Offer A: win back service customers

Three service messages for that store, drafted from gaps in its own Google reviews. The next slide shows it in full.

Offer B: trade-ins at service

A trade-in value for every service customer with an older car. A third of service customers want one, and 14% get one (Cox Automotive, 2026).

Offer C: follow-up that survives staff leaving

For stores hiring salespeople. 66% of dealership salespeople leave each year, and their follow-up leaves with them (National Automobile Dealers Association).

Offer D: one view across stores

For the top 50 dealer groups, especially ones that just bought a store.

Each store gets an offer its own research supports. Where more than one fits, Offers A, B and C are split evenly, so we learn which problem dealers answer.

Part 3 · The outbound stack

The stack, in the order the work happens

Every step reads from one workspace per client: the offer, positioning, brand, what past campaigns taught us, meeting notes and this week's priorities. The CRM and the sequencer are whatever the client already runs: HubSpot, Salesforce or Pipedrive, and Smartlead, Instantly or Lemlist.

01
Find
Apollo for buyers by job title. Google Maps, through Outscraper, for local businesses
02
Find emails
Clay, or my own Python script, tries several data providers in turn until one finds a work email
03
Research
AI agents read websites, job posts and public registers for signals and the facts each email needs
04
Check emails
Two email checkers must both pass an address. Anyone already in the client's CRM is removed
05
Write
The lead's ICP, signal, gap and job title pick the angle. Variables, conditional lines and spintax put it into words
06
Preview
My own preview tool shows every email for every lead and flags broken fields
07
Send
The sequencer, on warmed inboxes, 30 emails a day each, with tracking off
08
Replies
n8n sorts each reply and routes it. I check every interested one
09
CRM
n8n creates the contact and deal in the CRM, with the lead's tier, signal and email version
10
Report
Weekly, in the CRM or Google's Looker Studio, by tier, signal and email version

Part 3 · Reply routing

n8n routes every reply by what it says

Interested

A deal in the CRM, and a Slack alert telling the sender to reply within the hour

Gives a date

“Try us after our renewal in March.” The emails stop, and the CRM reminds the sender to write again 60 days before March

Wrong person

The company goes back to step 1 to find the right buyer

Away, and names a colleague

We find and check the colleague’s email and add them to a short campaign that says who sent us

Unsubscribe

Blocked in the sequencer and the CRM the same day

No reply

The account waits on the watch list until a new signal gives a fresh reason to write

Slack also alerts the sender when someone scans the code on a Tier 1 map, and the sender calls that day. It alerts the team when a campaign pauses itself or finishes.

Part 1 · How the emails go out

Deliverability and the emails are tested together, from the first send

Every campaign, on loading

Leads are grouped by who runs their email: Google, Microsoft, or a security service such as Proofpoint that screens mail first. Each group sends from matching inboxes. The three versions of Email 1 are split evenly inside every group.

Every week · Do the emails land?

A group passes with bounces under 2% and test emails in the inbox. A group that fails is paused and fixed, and is left out of the comparison.

Every week · What works?

The three versions of Email 1, compared on positive replies in the groups that pass. From week 2, also which step gets the replies: Email 2, Email 3, LinkedIn or a call.

Why keep all three running? About 950 people get each version across C2, C3 and C4. That shows a direction. It doesn't prove a winner.

Part 1 · Signals and measurement

Five public signals decide how warm a lead is. The weekly report shows which ones lead to meetings.

Signals, strongest first

  • A rival student-chat tool on the university's website
  • A new head of recruitment or admissions in the last 90 days
  • A job post for a recruitment or student ambassador role
  • A published fall in enrolment
  • A visa change in the countries it recruits from

How I'd judge month 1

  • Replies, positive replies and booked meetings, by tier and by signal
  • If Tier 3 books meetings as often as Tier 2, my tiering is wrong, and I change it
  • The target is about 20 positive replies from 3,000 people, the result SalesCaptain's own outbound package promises

Every campaign goes through three checks

An email counts only when two checkers pass itSome mail servers accept every address. I test each domain with a made-up address, and if it's accepted, I mark that domain as a risk.
Nothing sends until I've seen every email filled inMy preview tool exports the checked file, and only that file goes into the sequencer.
I check every reply marked interestedSending tools often call a reply interested when it isn't. Checked replies go into the CRM with their tier, signal and email version, and the sender gets a Slack alert to reply within the hour.
