Vanelo 60-day growth plan

You asked me on today's call to prescribe a 60-day growth plan for Vanelo. This is the plan. You can change every one of them in the calculator and watch the cost per paying founder, lifetime value and budget move.

Every default number on this page is either marked as an estimate or names its source. In week 1 I replace the estimates with Vanelo's real numbers wherever PitchHub has them.

1. The goal and where you start

  • Vanelo went live about two weeks ago and has 17 sign-ups. Vanelo has no free plan, so I treat all 17 as paying. A few may have come through an accelerator's free access, and week 1 confirms that.
  • PitchHub's year-one target is 2,500 founders, about 210 a month.
  • There is no ad budget today. Davies wants ads to amplify what already works, so the plan starts with a small ad test with a fixed budget.
  • Success at day 60 is two things. First, founders signed up and paying. Second, a repeatable system: email sequences, follow-up emails that move a lead toward paying (nurture), messaging and outreach routines that keep running after day 60.

This is what the plan projects for the base case. Base is the most likely case. The low case uses the weakest rate at every step, and the high case uses the strongest.

2. The five channels in scope, and the work that stays with your team

Channel 1

Paid Meta ads to your founders page

Facebook and Instagram ads (Meta) send founders to vanelo.io/founders, with a free first step such as the Fundraising Readiness scorecard or a live session with Davies. Your page already carries Davies's 2 minute 40 second video, so no new video sales letter is needed. The page does need some changes before ads send traffic to it, set out below.

Ads never go straight to the sign-up form, because a £240 a year product rarely sells to a stranger on the first visit. The page changes, the Meta pixel and the first set of ads are built in weeks 1 and 2. Ads run in weeks 3 to 8.

Channel 2

Outbound to founders

Four routes. Email to PitchHub's own founders, about 5,000 reached so far. Cold email to founders who are raising. LinkedIn from Davies's profile. Helpful answers in founder threads on Reddit and X, with no cold direct messages, because Reddit treats them as spam.

Channel 3

Outbound to partners

A list of about 150 UK partners: accelerators, incubators, university enterprise teams, angel networks, startup accountants and lawyers, and bank startup teams. Each gets an offer: a discount code for their founders, a co-hosted session with Davies, or a mention in their newsletter.

Partners decide slowly, so most of the result lands in weeks 5 to 8. The bigger value comes after day 60, when a partner sends every new cohort.

Channel 4

Search engine optimization (SEO)

Pages and free tools aimed at UK searches about SEIS and EIS tax relief, angel investors, pitch decks and funding stages. A young site rarely ranks within 60 days, so SEO adds 0 to 5 paying founders by day 60. Its value is the following 12 months. The 60 days build a SEIS eligibility checker, a valuation calculator on top of Vanelo's own valuation feature, and 8 to 12 articles.

Channel 5

Founder referrals

A paying founder invites another founder with a personal link. Once the invited founder pays, both get one free month. That gives up about £22 of revenue on each side, about £44 for each referred founder, and costs no cash.

We ask for the invite at a moment of value, not at sign-up. Examples are the first time an investor views a founder's data room, or the day their showcase goes live. Vanelo's developer adds the referral links in the app. If billing runs on Stripe, coupon codes can carry most of it. I confirm that in week 1.

Referrals launch in week 3 and run through week 8. They belong to the repeatable system because they grow with the number of paying founders.

Your founders page before ads

I looked at vanelo.io/founders on desktop and at phone width on 20 November. Davies's video is a real asset. These are the things I found that would waste ad spend if we left them as they are.

  • The video sits below the hero, so a visitor can sign up or leave without seeing it.
  • There is no button after the video.
  • The body has only two Get Started buttons. The second comes after nine long sections, which on a phone is well over 7,000 pixels of scrolling without a button.
  • On phones the header shows only the logo, with no button, and the hero headline runs off the right edge of the screen.
  • I found no Meta pixel in the page source or its main script. Microsoft Clarity is installed, so its recordings can show how far visitors scroll and whether they play the video.

Here are the eight changes I would make in weeks 1 and 2, before any ad runs.

  1. Put the video in the hero, next to or under the headline, so paid visitors see it first.
  2. Put a button directly under the video.
  3. Add a Get Started bar that stays on screen on phones.
  4. Add a button after every two or three of the nine feature sections.
  5. Fix the phone layout so the hero fits the screen.
  6. Add proof near the top: the £14m+ raised by PitchHub founders, the 5,000 founders reached, institutional logos and a founder quote.
  7. Add the free first step as a second button for visitors who are not ready to pay.
  8. Install the Meta pixel with events for page view, lead and sign-up, so Meta learns who converts.

I write the changes and the copy. Your developer ships them, unless you give me access to the site.

How the ads are built

Boosting one organic video or image gives Meta one creative and one signal, so Meta can only find the narrow group of founders who respond to that one piece. Meta's delivery system decides who sees an ad from the creative itself. The campaign therefore needs several clearly different ads, and Meta works out which founders respond to each.

The structure is one campaign and one ad set, with broad UK targeting, optimised for the lead event, and 8 to 10 ads. You already have most of the assets to build them from.

Founder video

15 to 30 second cuts of Davies's video and masterclasses, each opening with a different hook.

Testimonials

PitchHub founders who raised money, on video or as quote images with photos. We record them in week 1 where they do not exist yet.

Product screenshots and screen recordings

The data room, valuation range, investor list and investibility score.

The nine-question carousel

The founders page's nine questions, one per slide, such as "Is my business attractive to investors?" and "What is a sensible valuation for my business?"

Proof images

£14m+ raised, 5,000 founders reached and 120+ masterclasses.

Each ad speaks to a different worry: not knowing which investors to approach, guessing the valuation, getting documents ready for due diligence, SEIS and EIS, and getting seen by active investors.

Every week we replace the weakest two or three ads with new cuts from the same footage, because ads tire within about a week at this budget. Your video editor and designer make the ads from my briefs.

What stays with your team

The bootcamp roadshow, the podcast and YouTube channel, regular social content, exhibitions and speaking, pitch competitions, the course and bank workshops are ongoing work that PitchHub's team runs. I feed them assets, such as the ad creatives, the email sequences and the partner list. I do not run them.

How we use LinkedIn, X and Reddit

Today PitchHub posts on LinkedIn and Reddit and waits for founders to find the posts. The plan finds founders who are raising and starts a conversation with each one. The posts support that by giving new connections a reason to accept and reply.

I build the system once: the target rules, saved searches, keyword alerts, message and comment templates, and a daily checklist. Davies or a team member he names runs it every day, about 30 minutes, from Davies's accounts. I never log in as Davies. These channels count in the projection only if PitchHub names that person. The daily work is mostly replying, not posting: commenting on founders' raising posts, answering threads, replying to everyone who comments on Davies's posts, and following up in messages. Without a named person, these channels drop to Davies's normal posting with no daily replies, and the base case falls from about 92 to about 80 new paying founders.

LinkedIn, Davies's profile

Who: UK founders and CEOs of companies with 1 to 10 staff, founded in the last three years, who show a raising signal. That means a post about raising, pre-seed, SEIS or a pitch deck in the last 30 days, a place on an accelerator, or a first hire.

Warm first: Davies comments helpfully on a founder's raising post before he connects, so the request comes from a familiar name.

Connect: about 20 requests a day, with no note or a one-line note. That stays well inside LinkedIn's weekly limit. We use no automation tools, because LinkedIn restricts accounts that use them.

Posts: three a week, on fundraising lessons from 120+ masterclasses, founder stories and short product clips.

X, Davies's account and Vanelo's

We use public replies, not direct messages. X limits messages to people who do not follow you, and cold ones read as spam.

Each day Davies searches for founders asking about raising, such as "raising pre-seed", "SEIS", "angel investors UK" and "pitch deck feedback", and replies with real help. That is about 10 replies a day. His profile links to the founders page.

He posts once a day, reworked from his LinkedIn posts and the SEO articles. He answers every reply and quote on his posts the same day, because those conversations are where founders ask about their raise.

Reddit, a named account for Davies

Where: founder communities such as r/startups, r/Entrepreneur and r/venturecapital, plus UK small business communities. I check each community's rules on self-promotion in week 1, because some ban links.

How: detailed answers to fundraising questions, with no link in most of them. A link goes in only where the rules allow it and it directly answers the question. If a community allows it, Davies runs an "ask me anything" on SEIS and pre-seed raising, using PitchHub's £14m+ track record.

Finding threads: free keyword alerts such as F5Bot on "SEIS", "angel investors", "pre-seed", "pitch deck" and "fundraising UK".

No cold direct messages. Reddit treats them as spam and bans accounts for them.

Why it lasts: Reddit threads rank in Google and get quoted by AI answer tools, so a good answer keeps bringing visits for months.

The outbound volumes come from the size of the market, not from a number I picked. The main source is His Majesty's Revenue and Customs (HMRC), the UK tax authority. Total market. About 6,000 UK companies close an equity round each year, and about 7,000 to 7,400 apply for advance assurance. More founders try to raise than succeed, so I estimate the yearly pool of founders who are raising or preparing to raise at 10,000 to 15,000. Vanelo's Prepare features also serve founders who are 6 to 12 months from raising, which widens the pool.

Raising right now. A raise usually runs three to six months, so I estimate 3,000 to 5,000 founders are in the middle of one at any point.

Partner pool. The UK has at least 700 to 900 programmes and networks that could promote Vanelo. The Centre for Entrepreneurs counted more than 400 incubators and 300 accelerators in 2022 (Nesta reports the same network). The UK Business Angels Association counted 86 angel networks in 2023 (evidence to Parliament).

I found no reliable count for university enterprise teams, startup accountants and lawyers, or bank startup teams. My list of 150 is the most relevant of them: programmes for pre-seed and seed founders, angel networks, and the accountants who file SEIS advance assurance.

Cost, value and payback

Customer acquisition cost (CAC) is what it costs to win one paying founder. Lifetime value (LTV) is what one paying founder pays over their whole time on Vanelo: the monthly price divided by the monthly churn rate, plus the share who buy the showcase review times the review fee. Churn is the share of founders who cancel each month. Payback is CAC divided by the monthly price, before any costs of running Vanelo.

8. The 60-day timeline

Weeks 1 and 2: build and warm up

  • Replace the estimates with Vanelo's real numbers (the list in section 10).
  • Make the eight changes to the founders page, install the Meta pixel and build the first set of 8 to 10 ads. Your developer ships the page, and your video editor and designer cut the ads from my briefs.
  • Set up the secondary domains and six inboxes and start the two-week warm-up. Write the three-email cold sequence and the five-email nurture sequence.
  • Build the list of 150 partners and the partner offer.
  • Start the work that needs no warm-up: Davies's LinkedIn requests (about 100 a week) and helpful answers on Reddit and X (about 15 a week).

Weeks 3 to 8: run

  • Ads run at about £330 a week, £2,000 in all.
  • Cold email goes to about 1,800 founders, three emails each.
  • Partner outreach runs, with most promotions landing in weeks 5 to 8.
  • Week 3: referral links go live in the app. Founders are asked for an invite at a moment of value, and referrals run through week 8.
  • The first SEO tools and 8 to 12 articles go live.

Week 4: the first read, with a stop rule

I compare each channel's real numbers to this plan. For ads, the rule is simple. If the cost per lead is above £15 after £500 of spend, I change the offer or the audience once. If it is still above £15 after the next £500, the ads stop. At base, the ads pay back about three times against about £300 of year-one value. In the low case they lose money, which is why the read exists.

Day 60: the review

We compare each channel's real cost per paying founder to this plan. We decide which channels keep running and where any new budget goes. I hand over the sequences, messaging and routines as the repeatable system. The 60 days answer one question: which channels work, at what cost, before real money goes into ads.
