The billing story

Intro

Your pricing page opens with "Best in class products, transparent pricing." Billing is one of the product areas this role owns, so I treated it the way I would treat any product: what the merchant sees, the one line the team repeats, how a change ships, and how the field wins the cost conversation.

I wrote this from your public pages as they stood on 24 November 2026. It is a first draft to test against your billing data and sales calls.

What a merchant sees today

Three charges on two schedules. A merchant pays a monthly platform fee, a per-message usage fee and phone carrier fees. Usage on Starter and Growth is charged each time it reaches a threshold such as $100, and carrier fees come as a separate charge once totals are final.

A 2026 change most merchants will feel. Since 1 January 2026, undelivered messages are billed at the standard rate and replies carry a message charge. Your article estimates a 2 to 4% rise for most merchants.

No worked example anywhere. The help center explains each charge in its own article: plans, carrier fees, the 2026 change and legacy credits. None adds them up into one month's bill, so the merchant does that math alone.

Why it matters to you. Public reviews on the Shopify App Store and G2 name cost and billing disputes as a recurring complaint. Each surprise becomes a support ticket, and each confused prospect becomes a sales call spent on arithmetic.

The position

One line. With Postscript you pay for the messages you send and nothing you didn't ask for: no prepaid balances, no profile fees, carrier fees at cost.

Why this line. It is already true on your pricing page, which promises "No prepaid balances, expiring credits, overages, or auto-upgrades" and "Zero profile fees". Your pricing guide argues cost per message is the right way to compare platforms. The line turns those claims into one promise the website, the field and the invoice can all keep.

Launching a billing change

A billing change is a launch, because it changes what a customer pays. I would run every one through three tiers, so small changes stay fast and big ones never surprise anyone.

TierExampleWhat ships
1. Most merchants pay moreThe January 2026 changeHelp article and the bill page updated first. Email at 60, 30 and 7 days with each merchant's own estimated impact. In-app banner. Customer success talk track and field FAQ. Ticket review 30 days after.
2. One segment is affectedA new add-on fee, a carrier fee increaseEmail to the affected merchants 30 days ahead, help article, a short note to sales and support.
3. Nothing changes in priceInvoice layout, new payment methodRelease note and help article.

The rule that matters most in Tier 1 is the merchant's own number. "Your bill will rise about 3%, around $24 on last month's volume" lands better than an industry explanation, and it gives success managers one fact to repeat.

Field card: the billing objection against Klaviyo

One card per competitor, built for a rep who has two minutes before a call. Claims about Klaviyo come from your own compare page and pricing guide; anything that needs win data is marked.

Klaviyo in one line

An email-first platform with SMS added, strongest with brands that already run their email there and want one bill.

Why we win on cost

  • No prepaid SMS balance. Your pricing guide says Klaviyo requires SMS prepayment. [proof needed: deals where prepayment came up]
  • Zero profile fees, so a growing subscriber list doesn't raise the platform price. [proof needed: a merchant's before and after]
  • Carrier fees shown at cost on their own line, so a merchant can audit them. Your guide says Klaviyo bundles them. [proof needed]

Handling objections

"Klaviyo gives us email and SMS on one bill." Postscript now sends email too. Ask what they pay per text today with carrier fees included, then show the bill page with their own volumes.

"You started charging for replies." Show the merchant their own number. Postscript estimates the change at 2 to 4% for most merchants, and the bill page shows exactly where it comes from. [proof needed: how competitors bill replies and failed sends]

Questions to ask

  • What did your last three SMS invoices total, and could you predict them?
  • How much of your SMS budget is prepaid today?

How I'd measure it

  • Billing tickets per 1,000 active stores, before and after the bill page ships.
  • Share of trial stores that open the estimator, and their trial-to-paid rate against stores that don't.
  • Win rate in deals where cost was the stated objection, from call notes.

Appendix

The example's math

Messages: 40,000 × $0.008 = $320, plus 5,000 × $0.03 = $150. Replies: 2,000 × $0.008 = $16. Carrier fees: 40,000 × $0.00435 = $174, plus 5,000 × $0.00832 = $41.60. Platform fee $100. Total $801.60. All rates are from the pricing page. Replies are charged at the message rate per the January 2026 article; carrier fees on replies are left out to keep the example simple.
