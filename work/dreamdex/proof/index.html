A brief for dreamDEX

Product-Surface Brief: Porting the Five Pillars Onto the App

James Praise · Product Marketing Operator

Built entirely from dreamDEX's own public surfaces: the "dreamDEX is the endgame DEX" blog post and a direct check of the live app at app.dreamdex.io.

The brief

The Five Pillars, in a blog post

dreamDEX's blog lays out a real, sharp differentiation story, five pillars: permanent zero fees via a yield-based CLOB, native MCP server access for systematic and AI-driven traders, 1M TPS performance, algorithmic transparency, and a composable ecosystem. The core claim, verbatim: "traders no longer have to choose between the safety of self-custody and the performance of a centralized exchange."

5real differentiation pillars published in the launch blog post
1place they currently live: that single post
0mentions of any of it on the live app

What the live app says instead

Checked directly at app.dreamdex.io: three functional access points (Trade, Lend, Docs), wallet connectivity, a standard compliance footer. No mention of AI agents, MCP access, or the Agentic L1 narrative anywhere. No comparison to CEXs or other DEXs. No social proof, no Founding Trader numbers, no testimonials, despite a real, live Founding Traders program having launched in May 2026.

The story that would make a systematic trader stop and connect a wallet exists. It's one click away from the page where that decision happens, and it isn't on it.

Porting the story onto the app

AudienceWhat they need to see on the app itself
Systematic / AI-driven tradersThe native MCP server access claim, load-bearing, not buried, since almost no trading venue offers it
Traders new to onchainThe self-custody-vs-CEX tradeoff, explained plainly, using the blog's own "no longer have to choose" framing

Once Founding Trader numbers exist, they belong on the app too, the same "adoption is real" proof-point pattern already identified as underused across Somnia's broader public surfaces.

Why I structured it this way

A craft gap, not a domain claim

I didn't build this brief around derivatives strategy or order-book mechanics, that's not a gap I can credibly close, and I'm not pretending otherwise. What I can point to, precisely, is that a real, well-written differentiation story exists and hasn't reached the surface where a trader decides. That's a positioning and information-architecture problem, the exact skill this role hires for regardless of category, and it's the same motion behind the Dash0 comparison-page brief I'm working on in parallel this same week.
